# Module Overview — Cavnar AI

One section per product module: what it does, the key files, the invariants an audit checks, and what NOT to touch casually.

---

## Reviews

**Files**: `fetcher.py` (Google/Yelp pull), `analyser.py` (Claude sentiment/category/summary/severity/entities), `drafter.py` (AI reply drafting), `review_intelligence.py` (the consultant layer), `review_signals.py` (owner re-tags, the menu as dish vocabulary, review requests matched to reviews), `automation_trust.py` (when a star band earned and lost auto-approve), `client_api.py`/`mobile_api.py` review routes.

**Web inbox (friction #1/#7/#20, 9/25/26)**: the inbox leads the Reviews tab and the analytics sit under it, collapsed, loading on first open. A drafted reply has Edit beside Approve, and its text opens the editor — editing is never a Skip, so auto-approve trust never counts it as a "no". A filter or search over a partial page asks `/api/reviews/page` and replaces the list; new reviews go in at the top without a reload. `rvFocusReview(id)` (the `review/<id>` nav path, the bell, alert links, the diagnosis chips) opens one review, fetching it by id when it is not loaded.

**Flow**: scheduled fetch → `analyser.py` scores sentiment/urgency/severity and extracts the dish, role and daypart the guest named → `drafter.py` writes a draft reply → owner approves/edits/skips → posted back to the platform (where API access allows) or copy-pasted. Separately, at 6am daily, `scheduler.run_review_diagnoses` clusters the negative reviews and produces a root-cause read per cluster.

**Invariants (audit #10)**: a Google Business location is matched on `google_place_id`, never fuzzy name matching. A review's time for trend/period purposes is `review_date` (when it was posted), not `fetched_at` (when Cavnar AI saw it) — a backfilled batch of old reviews must not appear as "this week's activity." An unanalysed review (fetched but not yet scored) still counts toward totals — it just lacks sentiment/urgency until the analysis pass catches up. A drafted reply is never allowed to invent a commitment ("we'll refund you," "come back for a free X") the restaurant hasn't actually offered.

**Invariants (audit #13 — the consultant layer)**: there is exactly ONE review time axis, `models.REVIEW_TIME_AXIS_BARE`, and every window in every file uses it — there were three answers to "the last 30 days" and the same AI prompt once carried two of them. A pattern claim needs volume behind it on both sides (`MIN_CLUSTER_MENTIONS`, `MIN_TREND_REVIEWS_PER_WEEK`, `MIN_TOPIC_TREND_MENTIONS`), and a "concentration" that holds less than half its cluster is not reported at all. A rating direction carries a real confidence, computed by the same slope-agreement scorer `waste_trend` uses for waste dollars — never a bare monotonic check over three weekly means. A cause is only ever asserted from a stored diagnosis that cited real review ids; with no diagnosis, no cause is stated. The cross-module block distinguishes "no data" from "nothing notable", and sample data is refused rather than reported. A revenue figure is a labelled forecast, a range, shown with its inputs, and absent entirely when either input is missing. And a claim's kind (`ai_guard.CLAIM_KINDS`) travels with it to both clients, so measured, inferred and projected never render alike.

**What the review layer learns (memory audit 9/29/26)**: who approved a reply is recorded inside the approval (`reviews.approved_by` / `approved_role` / `approved_via` — `normal`, `view_as` or `rule`; under view-as, the admin behind the session). The drafter's examples and its OWNER'S EDITS note come only from the account holder's approvals or a login granted `permissions.REVIEWS_VOICE` ("Writes replies in our voice") — never an admin's, a view-as, the auto-approve rule's or a bulk approval (`models.reply_voice_sql`) — banded by rating (1–2, 3, 4–5; a short band borrows only from 3), edited replies only once any exist, chosen per review rather than once per batch. The owner's most-used templates for the band and the fixes the owner confirmed done on the review's own complaint categories go in fenced (`drafter.owner_templates_block`, `confirmed_fixes_block`); a draft that names a confirmed fix is always flagged for review, so bulk and auto-approve skip it. A regenerated draft counts as a "no" (`reply_draft_rejections`, 90 days, a hash and closed-vocabulary signals, never the text). Auto-approve trust counts only the account holder's approvals and is remembered: `automation_trust` keeps when a band earned it and on what, holds it on weak credit from auto-posts left standing a week (half an approval each, never toward earning it), and names a lapse (`/account/trust`) instead of dropping the band silently. The owner or a manager can re-tag a review (`POST /reviews/<id>/retag`, `review_signals.retag`): the row takes the correction, a re-analysis keeps it, and the analyser reads recent re-tags as this restaurant's examples, with the menu as its dish vocabulary (a guest's dish mapped to the one menu dish it names, the guest's words kept). A review request is matched to the review it produced (the guest's first name and last initial, within 14 days, one-to-one or not at all, at ingest), so the request stats carry a conversion. The diagnosis prompt carries the complaint slice (`review_intelligence.slice_context`: the shifts and people on those days against the window before, schedule edits, events and campaigns, the closers' notes, fenced), what was already tried on the theme (`tried_on_theme`) and memory; every read and diagnosis is kept in `ai_reads`, and a diagnosis's claim ("if the cause is right, cold-food complaints fall within two weeks") is scored at its horizon — "untested" when the advice was never taken, never "wrong". None of this learns from a restaurant that does not learn for itself (`models.learns_for_itself`: a demo or an account an admin excluded).

### The schedule pipeline (`schedule_engine._run_schedule_job`)

In order: the inputs (1) and the managers' shifts planned in code; the model call and the parse that salvages its answer (2); the ranked repair loop (3); one rule sweep (4); the score, the review and the save, then the quality gate (5); and, when the owner sends, the publish gate (6). A generation runs on the bounded pool under one wall clock.

1. `schedule_rules.build_constraints` gathers everything once: the roster (`staff_settings.roster`, deactivated names excluded), availability, notes, approved time off (a whole day blocks the date, part of a day only that part — `blocked_parts`, 10/3/26) and pending time off (a warning), per-person hours envelopes and daypart windows, minors, the rules, the role floors, and the tail around the week here and at the organisation's other sites (hours count toward the ceiling, rows toward rest and overlap — a sibling's shift is checked by time, not the whole date, since 10/3/26; every live published week overlapping seven days either side, then a week's unsent draft, then last week's punches — see *People facts in the schedule's rules*). Since 10/2/26 a weekday blocked only between two dates blocks just that week's dates inside them (`blocked_dates`), an hours window with `from` / `until` applies only inside them (`window_holds`), and an expired certificate no longer counts toward a role's requirement (`staff_knowledge.expired_certs`, names normalised by `schedule_rules.cert_key`; keyholder status is decided after it).

**Who manages, who closes, which roles are one** (schedule audit 10/3/26 F1): a manager is whoever the owner says runs the floor (Team, `floor_manager`), else automatically a floor-manager or owner title on their role, a role they hold or one worked in the last 8 weeks, or the floor manager certificate — never a department manager's title or a food-safety card alone; acting managers count on their dates; the rules screen shows "Managers: …" to confirm. Closers are per role: the last of each role with closers (the roles the owner chose) to leave is one of its closers, on until close plus the role's one "stays until close + N" setting; a list past 30% of the roster is flagged, and Team → Closers suggests the cleanup from the punches. Job codes are grouped into role families ("Server AM" / "Server PM" are Server) for the section cap, certificates, the owner's rules, floors (held on the code for each half of the day), closers and cross-training; held roles reach the rules, the generator and the solver, and a role written for somebody who holds none of its family is `role_not_held`. A trainee's training shifts are never coverage and are flagged without a trainer beside them. An Owner role is salaried-style (no overtime line, `salaried_cap`, 55h) unless marked paid hourly.

**Demand, budget and baseline** (schedule audit 10/3/26, workstream E). The numbers the draft is built to are made in code. *The budget*: the owner's labor target counts salaries, so the hourly hours budget is the target's dollars less the salaried staff's share of the week's trading days (`models.salaried_week_share`), bought at the measured wage (`labor.labor_rate_basis` — the same chain that prices the punches: POS pay, person and role rates, what a role's people make), saying when the wage is assumed and, past a quarter of the hours, that it is no ceiling to cut to (`budget_basis.trim_ok`); a closed date takes no hours and none of the week's sales. *The demand*: each date has ONE demand number (`schedule_economics.date_demand` — the owner's budget for the night, else the dated facts with last year's holiday night, else the forecast's measured effects; measured rain on a fresh forecast multiplies in; written back as the date's `lift_pct`, so the profile's demand level, the SPLH objective's expected sales and the requirements read the same figure); it scales the usual crew in SHIFT REQUIREMENTS (bounded 0.75–1.4, held to the sales-per-labor-hour target — `splh_objective.hold`, judged against the hourly target net of salaries), moves each day's share of the budget, and the score judges coverage against the same numbers (`requirements_by_date`). The hourly curve and every daypart split come from the ticket archive by business date (`schedule_economics.measured_sales_curve`; the captures and the nightly report's hourly split for dates it lacks); a weekday never measured has no daypart target. A night closing past 11pm carries a *late* segment (10pm to close): its own usual crew (the people held across it), requirement row, half-hour coverage check and sales per labor hour. Year-over-year reads a holiday's own night last year (several holidays per date kept apart), else the same weekday — never another one — moved by this year's trend, and is context behind the projection. Sales stopped more than 14 trading days stop a generation (`_demand_data_through`). *The baseline* (`labor.staffing_baseline`): the punches less the salaried, the published weeks for the people who never punch, the manager's settled adjustments; a role family the restaurant's own history has no crew for borrows from its peers (`intelligence.staffing`, per family). An owner's labor standard (`labor_standards`) replaces a role's number with its reason, the family's people shared across its job codes by largest remainder so the split sums to exactly what the standard asks (`split_people`, schedule re-audit 10/4/26 SQ-12). A soft "+1" held to the section cap is taken off before the firm crew is (SQ-10).

**The rules as the owner means them (schedule re-audit 10/4/26, lens RULES).** `manager_gaps` judges the day's open hours as well as its rows (open at 11am with the first shift at 12:30pm is 90 unmanaged minutes) and covers on one timeline across midnight (a Saturday manager's 10pm-6am covers the small hours of Sunday; `IncrementalSweep` passes the neighbouring dates' manager rows as `context`); a training shift and a manager in training ("Manager in Training", "MIT", a trainee into a manager role while training lasts) never count as the floor manager (`counts_as_manager`, `manager_role_kind` → "trainee"). Owner-operator titles ("Managing Partner", "Franchisee", a bare "Operator") count as owner; with nobody counted as a manager the roster note is soft to the repair passes but holds the publish (`ROSTER_BLOCKERS`). One reader of a job code's half of the day (`shift_quality.role_daypart`, shared with `role_family`'s word split) serves the sweep, the scorer's leader and strength checks, the solver, the requirements table, `models.leader_rule_daypart` and the floors. A floor is the one test `shift_quality.floor_shortfall` (by family, half hour by half hour through the core window — the sweep and the score agree). A closer is held to their role's own close (`closer_need`: the owner's end-time rule for the role's night, a "stays until close" setting, the punches showing the role ending earlier → the last of the role out, else the close) and the breach's severity is about the close only. Overtime past 40h moves to a teammate with room even when the owner's maximum is higher (`overtime_line` is the legal line; the maximum is a ceiling); an unset salaried cap is no owner cap (`Constraints.salaried_cap` None, `salaried_cap_set`); the repair passes hold the soft rules ranked above them (`_Repair(tier=…)`). The owner's staffing rules read ranges and exceptions ("Mon-Fri", "except Mondays"); one with two counts, both halves of the day or two roles is not guessed at — it goes back as a rule Cavnar AI couldn't read. A block on a half of the day (part-day time off, daypart availability) is any minute in its core window (`touched_dayparts`); a certificate expiring mid-week counts until it expires (`cert_expiry`); approving the only manager's drop says the floor goes unmanaged (`shift_requests.drop_manager_gap`).

**A manager every minute, planned before the call** (owner, 10/2/26 — the highest rule; `schedule_skeleton.py`, schedule audit 10/3/26 PR-1, P-8, D-4, D-5, PR-32). Erik's first week had no manager on any day — managers barely punch (Erik 1, Jim 0, Anthony 2, Andrew 10), so the history the draft copies never asked for one. Before the model call, `plan_manager_coverage` covers each trading day from the first person on (the opening time, or the earlier usual first start on file) to the last one out (the close plus the longest role's stay after it; with no close set, the usual last end) with real opener and closer shifts that overlap 30 minutes at the handover — never a block shaped to a gap. Its inputs in order: each manager's standing shifts (`Constraints.standing_shifts`, the owner's word, kept exactly); availability, time windows, time off and every legal limit (`Constraints.can_add` and `fillable` for every row; a salaried manager stops at their own cap, else the restaurant's `salaried_cap`, else 55h — never the 84h ceiling; an hourly one at the overtime line); the days and hours each usually works, from published weeks and punches (`history_rows` — a salaried manager's record is the published week); then a fair split ranked by room under each one's cap, then days worked — never "salaried first". When hours are short, every night's close is covered before any morning; a gap shorter than a shift is the manager beside it staying on. Acting managers (`Constraints.acting_managers`) take a date only where no manager can. A stretch nobody can legally cover is returned with why, named in the prompt and, where the finished week really has somebody on without a manager, in the review ("No manager can be on Wednesday 10/7/26 from 4:00pm to 10:00pm — Erik: on approved time off; …"). Managers with no standing shifts and no record are asked about on the generate screen (`manager_plan.question`: "Which days and hours do Erik, Jim, Anthony and Andrew work?"). The rows reach the model as MANAGER COVERAGE — ALREADY SCHEDULED, right under PRIORITIES, whose item 1 opens with the rule as 1a (it gives way only to a manager's own availability, time off and legal limits); a line under SHIFT REQUIREMENTS says manager coverage is not decided by that table. The model is told not to write them — code adds them (AI cost audit 10/7/26 #35; it was told to keep each exactly, and its copies were then dropped). `labor.generate_optimized_schedule` merges them into the answer by code (`merge_pinned`: a model row over a planned manager's shift is still dropped, an `item_dropped` quality event), and `_run_schedule_job` restores them exactly, carrying `"_pinned": "manager_plan"` — no pass removes, re-times or re-assigns a pinned row (the parser's close cap never cuts a manager staying until the bartenders' hour after close). A redo plans only its days, against the kept days' rows.

2. **The prompt, the call and the parse.** The prompt is one user message in three blocks (`schedule_prompt`, schedule audit 10/3/26 C1): the standing instructions — identical for every restaurant and call: how rules are marked [HARD]/[SOFT], how coverage is counted, the defaults (ranked last), the output contract, one worked example and the ranked PRIORITIES (1a a manager on the floor every minute, 1b availability, time off and the rules, 2 shift requirements, 3 leadership, 4 the hours ceiling, 5 quality preferences, and the rank of every channel the owner's words arrive by); THIS RESTAURANT'S WEEK — identical for every call of one generation: the owner's standing rules in one block, the managers' planned shifts as MANAGER COVERAGE — ALREADY SCHEDULED (`schedule_skeleton`, below), the rules tagged as the code checks them (`schedule_rules.prompt_block`), one ROSTER line per person (can work, availability, hours with the code's own maximum and overtime line and hours already published per payroll week, score, experience, closes, usual pattern, wants, reliability — nobody capped away) and the context; and THIS REQUEST — its dates, their SHIFT REQUIREMENTS (per date × daypart: people per role, the day's hour target — the one hours figure — the demand level and leader need the scorer uses, why a number moved; `schedule_requirements`), its share of the hours and what the rest of the week already gives each person. Cache breakpoints after the first two blocks: a generation's slices, retries and rewrites read them from the cache.

**The route** (AI orchestration, owner decision 3, 10/7/26 — "Sonnet 5.5 first if the eval clears 80%, hard weeks straight to Opus"; PROMPT_LIBRARY → *Schedule generation* has the eval): every call of a generation runs on the route in force, `schedule_engine.schedule_route`, on the `ai_workflows` `labor_schedule` ladder — T3 Sonnet 5.5 first, T4 Opus 5.5, both thinking at medium. The pre-router (`hard_week_reasons`: a major holiday, a dated closure, a roster over 60, more than 12 owner standing rules, the restaurant's first generation, a week whose last run escalated) starts a hard week on T4. The quality gate's model rewrite — only after its fill in code did not settle the gate — is the escalation to the next rung, with the gate's reasons as its notes; a week already on T4 rewrites on T4, and a run at its calls or dollar cap keeps its draft. `SCHEDULE_MODEL` pins one model and skips the ladder. The job is one `ai_runs` row (start and final tier, the escalation, the gate's reasons and outcome, the cost — every call's `ai_usage.correlation_id` is the run id), kept by `schedule_engine._in_schedule_run` and written through the orchestrator's record path; `schedule_model_calls.tier` records each call's tier, and the call planner (`call_cost_model`, `rows_per_call`, `call_seconds`) reads the measured costs at the route's model and effort.

**The call** (`labor.generate_optimized_schedule`, schedule audit 10/3/26 PR-6, PR-26, PR-29, PR-30): the route's model (Opus 5.5 before 10/7/26, `ai_utils.MODELS["schedule"]`, which the `SCHEDULE_MODEL` pin still sets) with adaptive thinking (summarized) at the route's effort, `medium` (`labor.SCHEDULE_EFFORT` on the pinned route; high until 10/6/26), `max_tokens` 64,000 shared by the thinking and the answer (`SCHEDULE_MAX_TOKENS_THINKING`), streamed (`ai_utils.create_with_retry` collects the stream into one message; the job's `deadline` cuts one still writing), the system prompt `labor.SCHEDULE_SYSTEM_RULES`, the two cached blocks (`cache_control` ephemeral, `schedule_prompt.request_content`) and the generation's own enum schema as `output_config.format`. Only a `SCHEDULE_MODEL` override to a model before the 5.5 generation runs the old shape (16,000 tokens, no thinking, no effort — `labor.schedule_model_thinks`). What the model is told is in `PROMPT_LIBRARY.md`.

**The answer and the parse.** Every date the model reads is a weekday and an ISO date; the summary's dates reach the owner as M/D/YY; the model answers the schema built for the generation (`schedule_output.schedule_schema`: the roster, its roles, the week's dates and quarter-hour clock times as enums — the same for every slice — rows grouped by date with no weekday or hours column, a note from a fixed list the employee reads; the shape without enums, then the CSV contract, only when the API refuses it). `schedule_output.parse_answer` hands the pipeline its CSV rows (hours from the times), keeps every complete day of an answer cut short and names the day it stopped in, and never reads a structured answer as CSV; a refusal stops the generation with a sentence (schedule audit 10/3/26 PR-12, PR-13, PR-15, PR-28). Every call's full input and answer is stored (`schedule_model_calls`) and linked to the saved week, for `scripts/schedule_model_eval.py` (PR-31). Three output contracts, one per generation (`SCHEDULE_CONTRACT`, default `schema` — AI cost audit 10/7/26 #69, #70): `schema` as above; `compact`, the same rows with one-letter keys (~24 answer tokens a row to ~30); `shape`, slots with a count and no names, whose people `schedule_engine.assign_shape_slots` solves with `schedule_solver` over the same rules before every repair and gate step — a slot nobody can legally work is left out and its date asked again. An answer not cut short whose JSON broke partway keeps the days before the break and only the rest is asked again (`parse_answer`'s `recovered`, #71). While a job's answer streams, the days it has finished are counted (`schedule_output.DayWatch`, once per finished day) into the job's `async_jobs.progress_json`, and the Building screen — web and phone — reads "3 of 7 days drafted" from the pending poll (#36).

**What each call is sent.** The calls are planned from the rows the dates usually carry (`schedule_engine.rows_per_call`: `CHUNK_ROWS_PER_CALL`, derived from the 64,000-token ceiling the model's thinking shares, a thinking reserve and the ~30 answer tokens a row costs under the compact schema (`OUTPUT_TOKENS_PER_ROW` = `schedule_output.ANSWER_TOKENS_PER_ROW_ESTIMATE`), held to the row tokens a call may write for its minutes (`ROW_TOKENS_PER_CALL`), and fewer once the restaurant's own calls show a row costs more, thinking included (`schedule_output.measured_tokens_per_row`); schedule audit 10/3/26 E-28): one call, else date slices, else each department in date slices — the kitchen first, then the front of house with every floor manager, which is told when the kitchen is on — each department call given only its own people's rules, floors and limits, and its answer kept only for its own people (E-29, PR-17). Every call gets every generation input as it is (the manager plan — every call is shown the week's planned rows, and labor merges each call's own dates' and people's — the demand, the budget, the generation id, the owner's instruction), and sees every row already on the week. An answer is salvaged, never thrown away (P-34): the days `parse_answer` read whole are kept (on the CSV fallback, the days written before the one it stopped in); when it was cut (out of tokens, out of the context window or out of time) the day it stopped in and the ones it never reached are written again smaller (dates in halves, then a date by department, then a department's people in halves, `MAX_SPLIT_DEPTH`; a generation makes at most its planned calls plus `MAX_EXTRA_CALLS` (8), retries and splits included); a day left empty, or carrying only planned rows, is asked for once more, alone. Days nobody on the roster can work are never asked for and are named at the top of the review (E-20). What still could not be written comes back as `unwritten_dates`: the rest of the week is saved, the gap is a ⚠ line that holds the publish gate, and no backstop staffs it.

**A redo, and a first week.** A redo — the owner's chosen days, or the quality gate's weakest — writes only those dates, with the kept days handed to the call as the rest of the week and the kept shifts next to them named for rest; the owner's reason (a chip, `REDO_REASONS`, and their own words) rides the draft's `instruction` (`with_redo_reason`: said once, markers neutralised, at priority 5 — P-9, E-21, PR-18). The kept days are the owner's rows, never model text (re-audit 10/4/26 PIPE-1, PIPE-6): only the model's lines for the redone dates go through the parser; the kept rows are added after it exactly as saved (`schedule_skeleton.kept_rows`), each pinned — `manager_plan` for a planned manager row, `kept` for any other — with a stale NEEDS REVIEW mark taken off (the final sweep marks what is still broken, with today's reason). Dormant people stay on the active roster after the model, so the owner's row for one is legal (PIPE-5). A redo saves only over the draft it read, checked inside the save's one write (`models.save_schedule_history(base=…)`, `models.DraftChanged` — PIPE-2): an edit to a kept day saved meanwhile runs the job again from the model's answer (`_redo_base_moved`, at most `REDO_RERUNS_MAX` times), an edit to a day being redone keeps the owner's edit and saves nothing. The draft, its review, timings, weather and economics and the superseding of the week's other unsent drafts are one write (PIPE-7); a failure after it finishes with the saved draft (`_finish_after_save`, PIPE-8). Rows on a closed date are never asked for or kept on any answer contract, and one there is a hard `closed_day` breach (`Constraints.can_work` refuses the date — PIPE-4). A restaurant with no shift history of its own but a hand-built team drafts from its Role floors and, where similar restaurants are measured, their staffing — labelled a starting point (E-30).

**One wall clock.** Every generation runs on one wall clock (`GenerationClock`, P-22): sized from its planned calls (15 to 40 minutes), each call waiting at most the time left, a stream still writing at the deadline cut and kept like a truncated answer, the job store reading the job dead past it — and a job read dead saves nothing. At most `SCHEDULE_GEN_WORKERS` generations run at once (`generation_scope`; owners' presses queue on `submit_generation`'s pool, the auto-draft claims its job and takes a slot, P-39). A job is judged by its own deadline and aged from the slot it took, never from the press it queued behind (`async_jobs.started_at`, re-audit 10/4/26 PIPE-9); one called dead while it queued is never run. A press joins a running generation only when it asks the same week, days, draft and instruction — any other is refused with the week being built, M/D/YY (`generation_request`, `busy_message`, UI-8). The auto-draft skips a week that already has a draft in force or went out, and auto-publish holds a week whose draft the owner edited (PIPE-3). A restaurant that turns Labor on from 10/7/26 — made with it, or switching it on later — has the auto-draft on by default, on its spread draft day (`models.labor_auto_draft_default`, `_auto_draft_on_labor_switch`; AI cost audit #73): never a demo, never a restaurant made before, never a setting somebody already changed; the owner sees it on in Account → Automation ("On by default once Labor is turned on; switch it off any time") and the Studio's AI tab.

3. **The ranked repair loop** (`schedule_engine.repair_week`, schedule audit 10/3/26 P-47, E-18, P-3, P-15, P-41, P-36). The passes used to run once each in a fixed order and undo each other (the top-up's rows the trim's first cut, the trim's cuts the manager pass's adds, the fill passes' breaches the person repairs). After the parse, the rules are read again (`_rules_after_the_model`: time off approved, a request made, a day or daypart marked unavailable or a person deactivated while the model wrote is taken in — the narrower reading kept — and new time off named in the review — P-41), one frozen context is built (the Shift Quality signals, last week's tail, the other sites' shifts and the sales curve read once; every read made through `frozen_read` happens once per generation, the labor calls' attendance, readiness and held roles included — P-36), and every pass is a stage at the rank of the rule it repairs (`REPAIR_STAGES`): legality (`fix_person_breaches`, then `apply_fixes` for a row a person cannot work), a manager every minute (`cover_manager_gaps`, after the plan's pinned rows), coverage (role floors, stations, closers), overtime (`rebalance_overtime`), the minimum hours (`fill_min_hours`), the budget (the closes that run late padded — `schedule_memory.pad_overruns` — then the section-count trim and `trim_to_budget`, skipped when the budget is mostly an assumed wage, E-24), and quality (the top-up to the SHIFT REQUIREMENTS — once, keeping its quarter-of-a-day cap — the stagger, the solver once, the optimizer on one search budget across the cycles, the owner's start and end times by role). A cycle runs them in that order and the loop repeats until a whole cycle changes nothing (`REPAIR_MAX_CYCLES` 4, `REPAIR_MAX_SECONDS` 120, the job's clock less `REPAIR_RESERVE_SECONDS` for the score and the save; a state seen before ends it); the solver and the top-up run once per generation, and with under `REPAIR_MIN_SEARCH_SECONDS` of loop time left the solver and the optimizer sit a cycle out (`repair.skipped`) while the hard-rule stages always run. A stage's change is kept only when nothing ranked above it got new or worse and no hard breach did (`_judge`: `schedule_rules.regressions`, by what each breach is about; a quality stage may not push the week further over the budget; a legality repair may cost a lower rank only when the week ends more legal); a pinned row and every row of a day a redo keeps stay exactly as they were whatever a stage hands back. The loop ends by checking the legality, manager and coverage ranks against the best state it saw, and runs the trim once more after the last stage that could add hours (E-18): a week still over says which rules ranked above the budget spent the hours ("The rules that come before the budget added 6h: a manager every minute 6h"). Each stage runs in its own try (P-3, P-17): one that raises is captured (`schedule_stage_<stage>`), said in the review (`stage_failure_line`), and — for a legality, manager or coverage stage — listed in `review.stage_failures` with `blocks_publish`, which holds the publish gate; an input the draft was made without is captured and named in one review line (`_input_failure_line`). Every row carries a stable id (`_rid`) from the parse to the save, so each fix line, trim and stagger points at its row's final place and a row the loop made and took away again is reported as nothing (P-15). `repair_rows` is the loop as one call (the model replay's default repair). Every stage is timed: `schedule_history.stage_seconds_json` / `total_seconds`, and the platform check warns when the p95 of recent generations passes 10 minutes (`ops.schedule_generation_latency`, P-24).

**The coverage, fill and trim stages.** Shifts are held to each role's close at the parse, and every stage after it by the sweep's `past_close` (soft, ranked with the close rules and identified by role and date, so a swap of people is no change and a later end is worse — re-audit 10/4/26 PIPE-10; a manager may stay as long as any role does). The stages are the role floors (`_ensure_role_floors`), kitchen stations and the closer repair at the coverage rank, the section-count cap and the budget trim at the budget rank, and — at the quality rank — a coverage top-up that fills only a role and daypart short of the SHIFT REQUIREMENTS the model was given (`week_requirements` → `requirement_index`: the usual headcount, floors, minimums and critical positions, demand-scaled where they are, plus the half-hour ramp; never against the draft's own average — schedule audit 10/3/26 P-20) — the labor budget is a ceiling, never a quota. Every pass that adds a person asks `Constraints.fillable` and `Constraints.can_add` (minors, rest with the published tail, the run of days, hours; under the overtime line first, past it only for a floor or station nobody under it can take), and every pass that adds, cuts or removes refuses a change that makes a breach about the day or the people touched new or worse (`change_regressions`: `schedule_rules.breach_profile` / `regressions` over the day and those people, compared by what the breach is about — a manager gap, a closer, a floor — never by row; P-2, E-1). A `_pinned` row is never changed, and with `only_dates` (a partial redo) no pass touches a kept day (P-10). The section-cap sweeps read past midnight (E-19); the cap trim never cuts under a floor or past a rule and names the night it left over (`review.cap_floor_conflicts`), and the rules screen says when floors exceed the section count (`floor_cap_conflicts`, P-29). Every fill-in and trim pass (role floors, top-up, section cap, stagger, budget trim) chooses among its own legal options the one that costs the week the least Shift Quality, scored with `shift_quality.LocalScorer` — which re-evaluates only the dates a move touches (the week-level measures every time); the pass's own order breaks ties and takes over past its time budget. Arrival times have one legal outcome and are enforced as before.

**The manager stage** (`schedule_rules.cover_manager_gaps`) is the backstop to the plan, ranked under legality and above everything else and run every cycle until a cycle changes nothing; no later stage's change that leaves a day with more unmanaged minutes is kept. Each stretch somebody is on with no manager gets one (schedule audit 10/3/26 E-12, E-13, E-16, E-17) — a manager already on that day runs on or comes in earlier (within three hours), or takes a second, separate leg (rest, overlaps and the day inside the longest shift checked), else a manager off that day comes in for a shift as long as the stretch, or the owner's shortest shift (`min_shift_hours`) when set; somebody standing in as the manager on that date (`Constraints.acting_managers`) covers a stretch no manager can. Candidates are ranked least tired (days in a row), then least disruption (run on, second leg, new shift), then fewest hours this payroll week — being salaried only breaks a tie. A salaried person is never taken past their weekly cap (`Constraints.salaried_limit`: their own maximum, else the restaurant's `salaried_cap`, else `SALARIED_HOURS_CAP` 55h); an hourly one past their overtime line only when no manager could cover the stretch inside it. Every change is judged by breach identity (`_Repair`: nothing about a person's legality or coverage new or worse, the day's unmanaged minutes down). What nobody can legally cover stays a hard, day-level `no_manager` — shown on the day (`review.hard_days`), never as needs-review on a server's row — and `result.manager_coverage.left` names why each manager could not (time off, their cap, days in a row) and who could act; `manager_coverage.shortfall` says it once for the week as a review line. The rule leads the model's instructions (PRIORITIES 1a; `prompt_block`'s line names the same rank), and every pass that changes rows is refused a move that opens a new gap.

**Owners and salaried managers are not held to the hours rules** (`schedule_rules.hours_rules_apply`, 10/7/26): no weekly ceiling, minimum hours, days in a row, consecutive days off, rest between shifts, daily overtime or overtime-line reserve, and the score's fatigue and minimum-hours dimensions skip them (`ShiftContext.hours_exempt`). A weekly cap the owner set (`salaried_cap`, or their own maximum) still holds; an hourly manager is held like anyone else, since their overtime is real money. Overlaps, time off, availability, roles and the minor rules hold everyone.

**Person repairs** (schedule audit 10/3/26, A2): one weekly maximum everywhere — `Constraints.max_hours` is the person's own maximum when the owner set one, above the ceiling too (that allows them the hours, overtime included: `overtime_line`, the sweep, the swap index's `caps`, the prompt). `rebalance_overtime` hands an over-the-line person's shifts to a teammate in the role's family or a role they hold (`held_roles`), as a second leg too, then splits a shift (a teammate runs on into its tail, or takes it as a shift of their own), then trims where the family covers the stretch, keeping each receiver's reserve for a payroll week that runs into next week (`payroll_tail_full`, soft); a minor's age-band weekly cap is a line too. `fix_person_breaches` cuts a minor past their end, before their start or over the day's or week's cap (the day summed over every leg) and covers the stretch cut off by the role's family, or moves the shift to an adult; a run of days past the rule is split day by day until it is legal. `fill_min_hours` then gives anybody under the minimum the owner set legal shifts — from a teammate above their own target, else added inside the hours budget — never worsening legality, the manager rule, coverage or overtime; a week-level score term (`shift_quality.week_min_hours`) counts the minimum hours given. A start before 5am is the business date's night everywhere the rules read times (`start_minutes`, `shift_span`, `_span`, `daypart_of`, `window_allows`), and a row's hours are its real length (`span_hours`: the 10/31/26 close is 10 hours). Every pass leaves a `_pinned` row alone.

**Kitchen stations** (`kitchen_stations.py`, 9/30/26; Jim Heflin at Simple EJ's: "just because a sauté cook works sauté doesn't mean the same cook can be on grill"). The POS knows one job, Kitchen; the owner lists the stations (Labor → Scheduling rules → Kitchen stations), which roster roles are the kitchen, which stations each morning or night needs (by day, with a count) and who is trained on each. A schedule row still names the role; stations are ASSIGNED after the draft by matching each daypart's cooks to its required stations, one station per cook and only one they are trained on. The loop's `stations` stage (`schedule_engine._ensure_station_coverage`, at the coverage rank, after the role floors) gives a station no cook on shift can take a shift for a trained cook who is free (the same legal checks as role floors), else leaves it as a gap the review names. The budget trim never removes the only trained cover (`kitchen_stations.protects`). The week's assignment (`schedule_engine.station_report`) rides in the review: the Schedule Studio shows each cook's station, and the staff app shows "On Grill" on their shift (`staff_schedule.shifts_for_employee`).

**Stagger and trim** (`schedule_economics`). Identical starts are staggered along the day's measured sales curve (the quality-rank `stagger` stage; never opening a manager gap or a half hour the ramp needs), and the week is trimmed back to the hours budget (the budget-rank `budget` stage, owner toggle): the ends of shifts first — the close stagger, first in first cut, at most 2h off a shift and never under 4h, someone else in the role staying through the hours cut (P-27) — then whole shifts, most discretionary first, never the last of a role out. The trim never makes a rule breach new or worse (the night's closer, a manager every minute, a floor, minimum hours — E-2, P-13), never cuts under the SHIFT REQUIREMENTS or their half-hour ramp (SQ-7), never touches a weekday where cuts measured worse (`learned_worse`) or a daypart that has gone wrong before (`outcomes_by_daypart` "troubled"), and never takes back a "+1" the reviews or the nightly reports asked for (`soft_requirements`, L-24); every change is reported (`trimmed[].kind` cut/removed) and a week it could not bring under budget says why once (`review.budget_conflict`, `budget_conflict_line`). Ranked under overtime and the minimum hours, the trim runs every cycle and once more at the end, with its scorer, rain and refusals. A role's arrival (`role_arrival_json`) is a clock-in lead before each person's own shift start, never a reason to move a shift: attendance reads a clock-in as late more than 10 minutes past start minus the lead (`attendance.clock_in_leads`), and salaried people are never judged on a punch (they don't clock in). The week's cost is priced with overtime at 1.5× past the ceiling, through one set of inputs (`schedule_economics.week_pricing` / `price_week`: each person's own rate, the 40h line per payroll week with the hours already published in it, daily overtime, salaried people free) that the generation, a reopened week and the live edit line (`cost_delta(..., pricing=)`, hourly hours with salaried hours apart) all use, so the three agree (schedule re-audit 10/4/26 SQ-6). The trim and the top-up rank a day on hourly hours against its hourly target (SQ-7). The Studio's labor % is `schedule_economics.labor_view`, worked out for the reader: all-in for the owner (hourly pay plus the salaried share of the week, against the all-in target and the all-in recent %), hourly pay for anyone else (against the hourly budget's share of sales), savings only on one basis (SQ-4); its hours panel is `hours_view` from the rule recheck — hourly by date and role, salaried hours apart, hourly people past the line (SQ-5). The prompt also carries what published weeks actually did by daypart, the rotation ledger, sales per labor hour, what a holiday did here last year, stated and learned staff preferences, who could hold a station, and the cohort's labor-hours-per-$1k ratio when the cohort clears the floor (`schedule_intel`, `intelligence`). A jurisdiction pack (`compliance_packs`) sets rule defaults under the owner's own values.

**The solver and the optimizer** are the loop's last search stages (the quality rank: the solver once per generation, the optimizer on one search budget across the cycles) — see *Shift Quality Engine* below, with the quality gate that may rewrite the weakest days once after the save.

### Data flow

|---|---|---|
| coverage | 20 | 70 |
| operational_strength | 18 | 55 |
| leadership | 15 | 60 (owner-written rules only) |
| demand_match | 10 | |
| labor_efficiency | 10 | |
| splh | 5 | |
| coverage_curve | 8 | 60 |
| experience_balance | 8 | |
| training_balance | 7 | |
| fatigue (week level, judged once) | 7 | |
| fairness | 6 | |
| reliability | 5 | |
| pairings | 4 | |
| stability | 2 | |
| cross_training | 2 | |
| preferences (week level, judged once) | 3 | |

| overtime (week level, judged once) | 8 | |

Coverage by the hour also judges every half hour of service against the restaurant's own measured sales curve (three or more same-weekday readings; `staffing_curve.half_hour_needs`): the busiest half hour needs 75% of each role's usual crew and each other half hour that crew scaled by its share of the sales. The capture is hourly, so the half-hour curve is interpolated between hour midpoints and every surface says so; the requirements table the model reads carries the same needs as a compact ramp ("Server 2 from 5:30pm, 3 from 6:30pm"). With no intraday data it is the owner's floors alone, as before. No requirement — whole-shift or half-hour, in the prompt or the score — asks for more front-of-house people at once than the section count (`staffing_curve.cap_requirement`, counting `foh_roles` together; the backstop and the repair loop count the same roles); where the history runs over the cap the review tells the owner the cap is probably wrong (`section_cap_conflicts`). Cross-training is judged role by role against the owner's target for that role (`role_cross_training_json`, set with the other per-role rules on web and iOS), else `CROSS_TRAINING_DEFAULTS`; 0 means the role is not expected to flex. Fatigue is a week-level measure (`WEEK_LEVEL_DIMENSIONS`): judged once per person per week, it stands in for the share of each uncapped shift it used to carry (`week_score_raw`), so a tired person costs the week once and a capped shift stays at its cap; it is shown under "Across the week" (`quality.week_dimensions`) and the repair loop sees it through `raw_score`. Minimum hours is week-level the same way (`week_min_hours`, weight 4): the hours the owner set as people's minimums, given; it withdraws when nobody has one (P-4). Preferences scores the daypart and weekly hours staff said they want, only for people who stated them — once for the week (the hours were charged again on every shift, SQ-24) — and the slots they keep dropping or claiming (`schedule_intel.behaviour_preferences`) at half the weight of a stated preference (L-19). **Schedule fix round 10/3/26 (D1b):** overtime is a week-level measure — the premium (the half of time-and-a-half past 40h in the payroll week, published hours included) as a share of the week's hourly pay, avoidable overtime (a same-role teammate has room) counting double the rest (SQ-25); kitchen stations is a dimension with a 70 floor, read through `kitchen_stations` exactly as the station pass reads it (SQ-26); training balance takes a cross-trained senior, or at a one-person station the manager on the floor, as the mentor (SQ-9); cross-training reads role FAMILIES and held roles, and a role nobody who works it can flex is not judged (SQ-10); fairness withdraws when nobody on the shift could be compared (SQ-11) and compares by family; fatigue and fairness read the published weeks before this one (`schedule_engine.load_ledger`: an average at the busy-shift ceiling or past the hours ceiling over the last four weeks, unless this week is the lighter one; the busiest shifts carried past a role's share — SQ-27); salaried rows are out of the day's and the daypart's hours and each person is held to their own ceiling (`Constraints.max_hours`, D-3); managers and salaried people are experienced by default, never turning the measure on by themselves (D-6); **The score is the objective (`schedule_optimizer.py`).** The repair loop's `optimizer` stage (the quality rank, one search budget across the cycles) is a bounded local search (add, retime/extend, replace, swap, a same-date trade, trim — generated first from what the week breaks of the scheduling memory, then from the weakest dimensions' facts, capped shifts first, with a move for every dimension — demand match, experience, reliability, pairings, preferences and cross-training had none — and, once those have nothing, the what-if's own swaps and replacements, so a better arrangement is made rather than reported) that takes each move that raises the week's value: its Shift Quality scored with that trial's own rows that will not stand and hard breaches, less LABOR_POINTS_PER_PCT per 1% of labor dollars it adds over the draft (`schedule_economics.priced_cost`: each person's own rate, overtime at its premium, salaried hours free; a saving earns nothing) and the rows it keeps that the manager is expected to change (`week_value`). Every candidate is legal before it is scored: whoever gains a shift is somebody code may choose that day and the row is legal for them with their week swept (`Constraints.fillable`, `can_add`), the whole week (an `IncrementalSweep`) makes nothing about a person, the manager every minute, a floor, a closer, overtime or minimum hours new or worse compared by breach identity with the week as it stands after the moves already taken (`schedule_rules.regressions`), nobody is newly past their overtime line (for somebody the memory says runs past their shifts, the line less that headroom), no close the memory says runs late ends earlier, no pinned row moves, the hourly hours budget (`schedule_rules.hourly_hours`, salaried hours never spent from it) is a ceiling, and the section cap holds (schedule audit 10/3/26 P-2, P-6, P-14, P-28, P-32, P-33, SQ-19, L-3, L-16). Every change is recorded with why (`optimizer.changes`), and what no legal move could fix is said (`optimizer.unresolved`, e.g. only one bartender authorized to close). A shift held by a hard breach is a problem ahead of every dimension (`HARD_RULE_PROBLEM_COST`), answered from the sweep's own violations by the generation's repair passes run on that date as one move each (`cover_manager_gaps` for a manager gap, `close_out_gaps` for a closer, `fix_person_breaches` for a person's breach), one more of a floored role, or the last shift run on to close; such a move is held as the generation holds it (nothing about a person, the manager or coverage new or worse) and not to the hours budget, a lower rank; and the search never stops at "target reached" while the week is held (schedule re-audit 10/4/26 SQ-3). `/labor/schedule/optimize` runs it on the week on screen without saving. The budget trim picks, among equally discretionary rows, the one the score can best spare. A draft that still breaks a hard rule or leaves a stretch with no manager while somebody who could put it right is free that day, or whose busy shifts are still capped by a staffing hole, a leader rule or a strength target a free qualified person could answer, has those days regenerated once with what was wrong named in the prompt (`schedule_engine._quality_gate`, schedule audit 10/3/26 P-43, SQ-20); the rewrite is kept only with no more hard breaches and no more unmanaged minutes than the draft — fewer wins outright, else a better score on those days with the week no worse (`_gate_keeps`). Which path settled it is an `ai_quality_events` row (`labor_schedule` / `gate_filled_in_code` or `gate_model_rewrite`), and the job's `gate` says it (`kept: "filled"`, `path`, `fill`). The review also names who the draft puts past the payroll-week ceiling, the days worth a standby, and the rows the manager is likely to change (`likely_edits`): first the ones matching an edit they keep making, then every row the predictor puts at 50% or more (and clearly above their overall edit rate), each with its likelihood and the rates behind it, once three finished drafts, 60 rows and 8 changes are on file.

**Who works each shift is solved (`schedule_solver.py`, #47).** The repair loop's `solver` stage (the quality rank, once per generation, before the optimizer): the draft's shape stays the model's judgment (which shifts exist, when, in which role) and the assignment is re-solved: a unit per drafted row (a drafted double stays one unit; overlapping legs are split), each person's legality from the same `Constraints` the sweep uses, and a complete search — most-constrained unit first, forward checking (conflicts, hours per payroll week under each person's overtime line beyond what the draft gave them, days in a row, a manager on the floor every minute anybody is — `Constraints.manages`, acting managers on their dates — and each role's closer, hard wherever the draft held them, a Hall check per role and date; a pinned row is never reassigned), branch-and-bound on an admissible bound, then large-neighbourhood rounds re-solved exactly — within the optimizer's budget (12s). Its cost mirrors the fixed scorer dimension by dimension in the scorer's own units (schedule audit 10/3/26 SQ-18: unrated people unknown, never the role's average; a bar never asks for more of the role than the shift has; strength per person against target ÷ crew; leadership credit; demand match against the restaurant's own ratings; training cover, experience, cross-training by family, reliability at the role's edges, preferred pairs split and per-role scores; fatigue, preferences — learned at half weight — minimum hours, the overtime premium and each person's labor dollars; the scheduling memory's learned-patterns measure per assignment); coverage, coverage by the hour, labor efficiency and SPLH are shape-only and constant to it. `score_rows` over every dimension, with each candidate's own breaches, less its labor dollars over the draft (`schedule_optimizer.week_value`) picks between the draft and the solver's weeks; a candidate that makes anything at or above the budget tier new or worse by breach identity, undoes a repair, or puts anybody newly past their overtime line is never kept (E-1, P-14). Its changes lead `optimizer.changes` ("Put Cat on Saturday dinner Server … instead of Ann — Cat is rated 5 to Ann's 2 …"), its stats ride in `optimizer.solver` (proved optimal or stopped on time, seconds, slots). A shift no legal week can staff — nobody legal, more shifts than legal people that day, more than the role can carry this week, or somebody the draft already overcommitted — is kept as drafted and named with why; the rest is still solved. Whether it runs is the week's arm of the live experiment (`schedule_experiments.py`, #50): a hash of restaurant and week (so a regeneration keeps it), recorded in `schedule_experiment_weeks`, pinned globally by `SCHEDULE_EXPERIMENT_PIN` or per restaurant — and, until `MIN_RESTAURANTS_PER_ARM` restaurants that may teach generate schedules (`powered`, the last 56 days), pinned to the experiment's `until_powered` arm, the solver, recorded as `pin_source` "underpowered" and never counted, the readout saying it is paused (schedule audit 10/3/26 P-21, L-11: the coin flip gave the one live client the model's assignment on three of its first four weeks for a verdict that could never arrive); the admin console's *Schedule experiments* page compares arms on draft acceptance (`schedule_versions.acceptance`), outcomes and the published version's Shift Quality (not an average over regenerations), and names no winner under 20 published weeks from 5 restaurants per arm. The interval is clustered by restaurant (memory audit 9/29/26): each restaurant counts at most its 8 most recent published weeks per arm (`MAX_WEEKS_PER_RESTAURANT`) and the standard error is the restaurant-cluster sandwich with a t on restaurants − 1, so one busy restaurant cannot narrow it into a false winner; only restaurants that may teach a learner are read (`models.learning_eligible`, never a demo's regenerations); and each week's verdict is stored (`schedule_experiment_verdicts`, written by the Monday 5am job of that name) so what the readout said on a given week can be read back. Owners never see an arm; an arm never adds a model call.

**Apply fixes, the what-if and the Studio re-score (schedule audit 10/3/26, D2).** Every pass that weighs trials against the rules keeps its sweep (`schedule_rules.IncrementalSweep`: a trial re-sweeps only the people and dates it moved, and answers exactly what `violations()` would — the same entries in the same order), so the overtime rebalance, the close-out, the manager filler, the person repairs and the role time rules no longer sweep the whole week per trial (P-37). `shift_quality.LocalScorer` builds contexts per date and re-scores only the dates a move touched (P-38); with `exact=True` its answer is `score_rows`' field for field. `apply_fixes` chooses only people code may choose (`Constraints.fillable`) for whom the row is legal with their week swept (`can_add` — under their overtime line first, past it only when nobody under it can take the row, never past their maximum), keeps a fix only when the week compared by breach identity is better and nothing else is new or worse (the manager's minutes included), never hands on a pinned row, leaves a breach about a day to the day, and takes `max_seconds` (P-2, E-1). `compare_candidates` (the what-if) holds every swap and replacement to every rule the week is when given its `Constraints` (`checked_with: "every rule"`), never offers a pinned row, lets rated and unrated people trade (the score decides) and takes `max_seconds` (P-33, SQ-19). The Studio's re-score keeps the week's inputs, live check, sweep and exact scorer for 120 seconds per restaurant, week, dates, hour targets and capability version (`schedule_engine.studio_prepared`; a save rebuilds them) and runs the what-if only when the request asks for it (`what_if: true`, P-25).

**What the draft learns (#39, #43, #46, #48).** `calibrate_weights` fits each outcome (coverage and no-show issues — only on nights `schedule_intel.watched_dates` says Cavnar was watching — the day's review rating, labor % against target) on every dimension at once (a standardized ridge fit), needs 8 published weeks, 40 shift outcomes and 20 shifts per dimension per outcome, keeps the fitted weight within 30% of the default, moves a weight at most 10% of its default per Apply from where it is now, and names the outcome that drove each change; the owner applies it (web and iOS). `schedule_intel.rotation_plan` plans the rotation per role from the last 8 published weeks — who is due a weekend off (most weekends in a row), who closes next (fewest closes for their shifts), who rests from closing, who works and who has the next holiday — and the prompt carries it as a preference below the rules; the fairness dimension judges the week against it (a person due a weekend off on a weekend shift while somebody in their role has the weekend free, a resting closer closing while the next closer has none). `schedule_economics.splh_objective` sets a sales-per-labor-hour target for every weekday's lunch and dinner from the restaurant's own record, raised by history labor % ÷ target labor % when it runs over its labor target (never lowered below the record); the prompt gets each shift's target and the hours its usual sales carry, the `splh` dimension scores each shift against it and withdraws without sales, and the review reports the week (`splh_report`). A restaurant with no history of its own (nothing published, under two weeks of shifts) gets a starting headcount borrowed from its category cohort (`intelligence.staffing`: people per role family and daypart per $1k of sales, cohort medians over at least `MIN_COHORT` restaurants, scaled by its own sales), marked "(borrowed)" in the SHIFT REQUIREMENTS table and said in the review; the scorer's typical headcount stays the restaurant's own. Below the floor, with no category or with no sales of its own, nothing is borrowed and the record panel says why.

Weights are per-restaurant editable (`quality_weights_json`); `scripts/schedule_eval.py` replays saved weeks through the current code and is the check before any change to scoring (`--calls`: the stored model calls behind a week and the model's own answer scored before repair); `scripts/schedule_model_eval.py` replays stored weeks across model × effort × prompt variant and is the check before any change to the schedule's model, effort or prompt. A shift is one date × daypart; a row counts in every daypart it is on the floor for (`present_dayparts`: its start's daypart, plus the other when it covers an hour of that daypart's core window), while its hours and week assignment count once. A profile's demand is settled per weekday from the restaurant's own sales (`profile_for_shift`), never the busiest of the days it covers. Experience withdraws when the history on file is too short for anybody to reach `EXPERIENCE_SHIFTS` and nobody is marked experienced (`staff_settings.experienced`). Fairness asks whether somebody on a closing, weekend or busy shift has more of that kind than their share among comparable people in their role. Labor efficiency treats under the day's target as fine while coverage (and the half-hour sweep, where measured) holds its critical floor — a thin spot above the floor is coverage's to charge, not a second penalty (10/3/26 SQ-31). Invariants that hold everywhere:
1. **No data → `None`, never `0`** — a dimension with nothing to measure withdraws and the rest renormalize. (A leadership dimension with zero ratings used to score `0` and cap otherwise-great shifts at nothing; fixed.)
2. **A critical dimension under its floor caps the shift at its own score** — never an arbitrary extra penalty stacked on top.
3. **Confidence is tracked separately from score.** A shift graded from 2 rated employees out of 9 says so; it isn't hidden inside the number.

`SUBSTANTIVE_DIMENSIONS` gates against fatigue-alone producing a score. What-if swap evaluation (`_SwapIndex`) only considers same-role swaps, bounded by `MAX_CANDIDATE_EVALUATIONS`, for tractable performance on a real week.

**Leadership, strength, coverage and caps (schedule audit 10/3/26, SQ-1–SQ-32 D1a).** The critical floors are per profile (`ShiftProfile.floors` over `CRITICAL_FLOORS` — coverage 70, strength 55, leadership 60, coverage by the hour 60; 0 = never caps; stored in the profile's config and round-tripped by `profile_from_dict`/`profile_to_dict`). *Leadership* gives each check credit for what it found (qualified ÷ asked for; the profile's "somebody able to run it" all or nothing) and is the weakest check on a scale where nobody qualified is `LEADER_MISS_SCORE` (50) and met is 100 — one missing leader holds an owner-ruled shift at 50 whether one rule applies or four (it was rules met ÷ rules applying: Erik's "25 / 70" and "0 / 70"); a miss says `nobody_on`, `not_qualified` or `short`. A rule naming no daypart binds only where its role works (`leader_rule_applies` over `role_runs`, the requirement grid — the prompt's requirements table asks the same function); a role nothing on file places anywhere binds every daypart as before. Managers, and acting managers on their dates (`signals["managers"]`, `["acting_managers"]`), run the shift the built-in profile asks about. A rule needing no score (authorized to close, a headcount) is scored with nobody rated. *Strength* is the average of each role's rated people against the target ÷ the full crew it was set for (`strength_crews`: the most of that role any shift the target governs needs; with nothing on file, the fewest who could reach it) — unrated people are a named blind spot, never 0, and an extra body never buys strength (the optimizer no longer adds people for it). *Demand match* is judged against the restaurant's own ratings per role (`role_ratings`; a busy shift wants its role's average plus `DEMAND_BAR_OFFSET`, never past what its best people could make; withdraws under 3 ratings), scored on the distance under the bar so a scale shifted by the owner moves nothing. A role's AM/PM job codes are one role in leadership, strength, demand match and coverage by the hour (`role_family`, the restaurant's `role_families` map first). *Coverage by the hour* holds the owner's floors (or whole-day minimums) over the daypart's core service window, not to close (`floor_window` / `floor_slots` / `floor_shortfall` — the one floor test `schedule_rules._coverage_violations` is to call); the measured sales curve still judges every half hour of the daypart. *Hard rules:* a hard breach the sweep found (`signals["hard_breaches"]` — `schedule_engine.hard_breach_map`, or violation dicts / breach ids — placed on the shift it is about by `place_breaches`; a person's week-long breach lands on the shift that tips it) holds the shift at `HARD_BREACH_CAP` (50, `capped_by: "hard_rules"`) — a fixed ceiling, not a dimension, so a clean shift scores as before — and holds the WEEK too: any hard breach on a scored shift scales the week score into [0, `WEEK_HARD_BREACH_CEILING`] (64, under "fair", so it reads "weak"; scaled, not clipped, so a move that fixes one breach of three still scores as a gain), `raw_score` included, and the week carries `held_by` (the first breach, how many, the ceiling) as its first weakness (`week_held`; schedule re-audit 10/4/26 SQ-2: a Saturday-dinner manager gap read 92 "excellent"). Scored on rows a pass changed (the what-if's swaps), a day's breach still holds and a person's goes with the person, so no change is credited with lifting a cap it did not lift; the finished rows are always swept fresh. *What the owner reads:* an expected daypart with nobody written says "No shift written for <day> <part>" on its cap; every counting dimension is customer-facing and carries its floor; every `below_profile` line carries `held_by`, `capped_by`, `no_shift_written` and a `reason` (the cap's text or the costliest weakness); each recommendation carries the points the week gains when it is fully done (`points_if_fixed` over the week's own aggregation; `recommendation_details`, `recommendation_points`, and `points` on the served `recommendation_items`), most valuable first, at most two of a kind; confidence carries a ranked `breakdown` and its `top_reason`, and built-in profiles re-levelled by the restaurant's own sales are not charged as defaults.

### Operational Score
Per-employee 1–5 rating (`staff_capabilities`), owner-set, keyed by employee **name**, with the person's `person_id` stamped beside it (`people.NAME_STORES`) so a rename or merge carries the rating. Feeds `experience_balance` and `leadership` in Shift Quality, and is exposed to Ask Cavnar's `read_team` tool. Dormant until someone is rated — a fresh account has zero effect on scheduling until the owner engages with it. Role matching is case-insensitive (`_same_role`/`_role_lookup`) — this used to silently disable every role-based rule.

### People, shifts and attendance (memory audit 9/29/26)
**Identity.** `people.py` owns who a person is: one row per person (`people`), every spelling and every POS id kept (`person_aliases` — each adapter emits `employee_ext_id`, RPOWER its payroll id too). `resolve_rows` gives every incoming row its person — the POS id first, then RPOWER's payroll id, then the exact name key; a similar name ("Kim T." / "Kim Tran") becomes a question for the owner (`person_questions`, Home's `people:identity` item), never a merge on a guess. `merge_people` / `rename_person` re-point every name-keyed store (`people.NAME_STORES`) and fold two rows in one slot; `person_merges` records what moved and `change_log` has the row. `stamp_person_ids` keeps a `person_id` beside the name everywhere. Roles a person was promoted or trained into (`add_role`, `held_roles`) reach the roster and cover suggestions; who took a cover when asked and guests naming staff are `person_signals` (a mention is proposed until the owner confirms it; the manager's word beats the inference). `auth.py`'s DDL comment "seven tables keyed by staff name" predates this — `NAME_STORES` is the list. Re-audit 9/29/26: a merge can be undone for 30 days (`unmerge_people`, the team's "Undo merge"), and a departed employee's record erased on the owner's word (`erase_person`, the person sheet — off the roster and without a login, their name typed back); the draft's learned patterns and their dismissals are in `NAME_STORES` and re-keyed with the person. Standing schedule patterns (`schedule_versions.refresh_standing_patterns`) stay retired once reversed twice inside eight weeks, go dormant while their person has no shifts, count only the manager's own hand as confirmation, and headcount kept once it works (`learned_headcount_adjustments`).

**People facts in the schedule's rules (schedule audit fix round 10/3/26, F2).** `people.identity_index` maps every spelling of each roster person (alias, old POS spelling) to the roster's own; `schedule_rules.build_constraints` files every per-person input under that key (`Constraints.key`) — availability, time off (whole days and `blocked_parts`, part of a day off: `time_off` start/end time or daypart), notes, holds, closer flags, certificates, station skills, salaried entries, and the facts F1 keeps per person (who runs the floor, acting managers, standing shifts, held and worked roles, trainees, closers per role) — and names a fact whose name matches nobody (`Constraints.unmatched`, the review's `unmatched_names`). Two roster names an open "same person?" question joins are one person in the sweep and `can_add` (`sweep_key`) until the owner answers. The scorer's per-person signals are re-keyed the same way (`schedule_engine._reconcile_to_roster`). Salaried entries are linked to their person on save and matched through every spelling (`models.salaried_keys`), and the settings warn about one that matches nobody (`models.salaried_matches`). `staff_settings.roster` is one entry per person with the role worked most over their last eight weeks; `dormant_people` (no shift in six weeks, nothing on file saying they are still here; never a manager of any kind — a department's manager too, or anyone on a manager's shift of a published week in the last year (`DORMANT_MANAGER_PUBLISHED_WEEKS`): managers barely punch — nor salaried people, standing shifts, acting managers or trainees) fills `Constraints.dormant` as the last step of `build_constraints` — never chosen by a fill, left off the model's roster and the open-shift broadcast (`shift_requests._who_could_take`, which now goes round instead of alphabetically), "Not worked since M/D/YY — deactivate?" on the team page. Every unconfirmed staff note is read by the hold reader into `Constraints.note_caution` (`person_note_holds.caution`). Owner-only staffing rules constrain the draft with their words kept private, and a rule about two people is a pairing (`schedule_rules.parse_pair_rule`, `pairs_with_rules`). Ratings can be per role (`role:<family>`, read by the scorer and the solver for a row in that role) and ask for a re-rate after 90 days. The usual pattern carries average hours and usual starts (`models.usual_pattern`), scored by stability and told to the model in each person's ROSTER line (USUAL: "Fri nights, ~32h a week, starts 4:30pm"); full-time means `full_time_min_hours` when no minimum is set. The fairness ledger and the rotation read a close across midnight and fall back to punches week by week (`schedule_intel.record_weeks`); the tail around a week (`schedule_rules._published_tail`) is every live published week in a seven-day window either side, then a week's draft in force, then last week's punches, and the organisation's other sites (`org_sibling_ids`) matched through identity and checked by overlap and rest, not by date.

**Shifts.** `shift_facts.ingest` is the one way shifts come in — the POS sync (`pos.save_synced_shifts`), an owner's upload and an admin's (`client_api._do_upload_data`) — with the POS rule for all three: inside the new rows' dates they are the record, outside everything is kept (a two-week manual upload used to replace a year of history). `client_data.shifts_csv` is still written for the labor analysis and every older reader; `shift_facts` holds the same shifts one row each, with the person, the source, whether the source carried a real schedule (`scheduled_hours` NULL when it did not — RPOWER, Square, Clover and Toast without one: a copied actual is not a schedule) and pay. Readers that ask about a person over time — reliability, tenure, usual days, mentoring, the overtime metric — read it with an explicit window. Raw rows 3 years, then per-person quarters in `person_quarters`, kept forever.

**Attendance.** `attendance_events` is one row per scheduled shift somebody watched — on time, late, no-show, called out (with the notice it gave when the shift requests show it, `notice_minutes`), left early, covered, excused (a drop a manager approved that nobody claimed — never a no-show; `shift_requests.shift_release` is the one reading of what the requests say, shared with the live check, schedule audit 10/3/26 E-5/L-18) — from, strongest first, a manual correction, a closer's confirmed callout, the live clock-in check, the nightly join of the published week against the punches once the POS day is final (`join_published`, the only source an RPOWER restaurant can have), and — weakest, since 10/2/26 — the employee's own "running late" (`self_report`, minutes late = their ETA; it never fills a measured row's minutes); a weaker source never overwrites a stronger one. A running-late report also holds the coverage check's "hasn't clocked in" issue until start + ETA + 15 minutes (`staff_comms.late_hold`), or adds the ETA to it when it is already open (`issues.note_running_late`). Reliability (`staff_settings.reliability`), attendance by weekday and standby days (`schedule_learning`), the prompt's no-show block and the reliability dimension read `attendance.reliability_events` through ONE weighted reader (`staff_settings.attendance_events` → `attendance_tally`: one 360-day window, one 90-day half-life, one smoothing toward the restaurant's base — L-17): a person nobody watched is unknown, never reliable (`shift_quality.dim_reliability` withdraws; a person record says `known: false`). Three rates: `no_show_rate` (reliability — a call-out weighs by its notice, `staff_settings.miss_weight`), `absence_rate` / `call_out_rate` (standby planning — every absence in full, L-18) and `late_rate` (over clocked shifts only, said from 6 of them; `late_risk` at 25% puts the person in the prompt's LATENESS block and costs a shift where they alone open or close their role's day — D-44). Excused and covered shifts are not owed, so nothing counts them. The `people_nightly` job (5am CT, bounded and resumable) runs the join, the cover and mention signals, the standing patterns and the quarterly rollups.

**Notes and patterns.** A staff note carries its own dates ("noted M/D/YY, ends M/D/YY" in both prompts); an ended note drops out and one nobody has confirmed in 90 days is an action-queue item (`staff_note:stale`). A retime or role change a manager keeps making becomes a standing pattern (`schedule_versions.refresh_standing_patterns`; two reversals retire it, a conflict between editors goes to the owner, `make_rule` turns one into availability with its author and a `change_log` row — or, since the schedule audit of 10/3/26, a headcount the manager keeps adding into a role floor and a start or end they keep setting into a role time rule the draft is retimed to and checked against). A standing pattern keeps its evidence (weeks tested, weeks kept, when a manager's hand last confirmed it) and decays without that hand: one half-life on (120 days for a person, 180 for a headcount or time) the next draft leaves it out once — the manager putting it back confirms it, a week sent without it retires it; two half-lives unconfirmed retire it; the owner can say keep it or let it go. An admin's view-as save is stamped (`schedule_versions.saved_authority`): its changes are taken out of the week (the rest of the week still teaches) until the account holder adopts them (`adopt_admin_saves`), and the same holds for an admin's pattern dismissal (`schedule_intel.adopt_admin_dismissals`). A week the automatic publish sent with no edit (authority `system`) is nobody's acceptance and no learner's evidence; a change saved after a week went out is a reaction, never a habit; the owner's redo of some days and a discarded draft are kept (`schedule_rejections`) and a redo never hides the edits made before it.

**The scheduling memory (schedule audit 10/3/26 L-29, H2).** `schedule_memory.py` is the one place that says what a restaurant's scheduling has learned, how sure it is and when it was last confirmed. Its log, `schedule_observations` (raw, 400 days), holds every atomic fact with its origin (manager, cavnar, staff, system), phase (pre_publish, post_publish, as_run) and authority: the edits, redos and discards H1 records, and what was MEASURED — the punches beside the plan per slot, a role the punches left short, the daypart's labor against target, a shift that ran late, overtime worked, the week's Shift Quality as it ran beside as planned (a measured fact is re-recorded in place by its `fact_key`). The memory, `schedule_memory`, is one row per fact with its evidence (opportunities and hits, misses by hand, the last hand confirmation), a confidence (Wilson × recency, and for a manager's habit × the decay since a hand last confirmed it), a status — candidate (two weeks and half their opportunities, from the manager's own pre-publish hand, the restaurant's own scheduling before Cavnar AI drafted it, or the floor), active (confidence 0.6: a soft cost in the passes and one short prompt line), rule (the owner made it one), retest, retired (reversed twice by hand, faded, no longer seen, or let go), dormant — and an enforcement (prompt, soft, hard). The nightly job (`strategy_jobs.run_schedule_memory`, 6am, bounded and resumable) rebuilds it from its sources (`consolidate`): every standing pattern carried in whole; openers per role and weekday; each server's usual section; who keeps running into overtime and the headroom to leave them; closes that run past their end; teams whose shared shifts ran well (sales per labor hour against the same night's median, no issue on a watched night, no poor review placed on it — a learned "keep apart" never applied on its own); weekdays the owner keeps redoing; and the other learners' facts (staff preferences, attendance, how each daypart went, who could hold a station) held here as their own learners decide them. A draft choice the manager merely kept keeps a habit alive and never starts one; an admin's word and Cavnar AI's own changes never count as the manager's. Generation refreshes the pattern facts before it reads (`consolidate(only=("patterns",))`), hands the active ones to every pass as `signals["learned"]` (`enforced_signals`; their meaning for any pass is `misses` / `learned_cost` / `slot_cost`), pads the closes that measurably run late where legal and never past the owner's after-close allowance for the role — the rest is left with why, theirs to raise (`pad_overruns`, `schedule_rules.close_cap_minutes`, re-audit 10/4/26 PIPE-10) and tells the model everything learned in ONE budgeted, relevance-ranked block (`prompt_lines`, with the engine's other learned blocks as its sections — L-28). The owner keeps a fact, lets it go or makes it a rule (`owner_answer`: a pair → their pairing, an opener → a standing shift, a closing overrun → an end-time rule; `GET/POST labor/schedule-memory`). Measured server performance is offered as a rating only the account holder confirms (`suggested_ratings`, `confirm_suggested_rating`; L-23, D-27) — never in a prompt. **What published weeks did** (`schedule_intel.record_outcomes`, L-12, L-13) now records the punches beside the plan — hours and people worked, shifts missed, late, left early and run late, and the daypart's own labor cost and labor % (each punch at its own rate, split at 3pm, over the daypart's measured sales; the day's `labor_pct` is no longer read as a daypart's) — and the Monday job scores each ended week as it ran (`record_as_run_quality`) and reads every recommendation the manager carried out against it (`measure_recommendations_as_run`, L-34; three more recommendation shapes are read as carried out by the edit itself). A strong calibration nobody applied waits in the action queue (L-14). **Re-audit 10/4/26 (LEARN):** a pair's polarity is its meaning in every pass — the owner's Keep on a learned keep-apart keeps the two on different shifts in `misses`, the solver's `team_cost`, the optimizer's moves and the score (named nowhere the team reads); only an account holder's answer is the owner's (`owner_said_authority`, `schedule_versions.confirm_standing` / `owner_said_pattern` / `OwnerAnswered`), and the owner's Keep holds a habit under the line; a padded close is measured against its unpadded end and an overtime headroom by the run past the plan, so neither fix erases its own evidence; a rule the owner removed goes back to the evidence; teams pass Fisher's test against the shifts without them, Holm-corrected over every group (`significant_teams`); what the punches show renews an opener under Cavnar AI's drafts (`kept_dates`); learned closers and the rotation's closing queue are the owner's chosen closers (`schedule_rules.chosen_closers`).

### What staffing hears from the rest of the product (memory audit 9/29/26)
`staffing_signals.trim_guard` is the check every trim makes first — Home's `trim_day`, the labor read's trims, the DSR's `control_hours` action and the pre-dinner cut: a live guest-text campaign to fill that night suppresses the trim and says why (a scheduled post never does — re-audit 9/29/26, CROSSMODULE-7); a service, wait or floor-staff complaint cluster on it keeps the trim with a caution and a lower rank. The schedule prompt carries WHAT THE LAST NIGHTS SHOWED (`last_nights_block`: four weeks of nightly-report metrics per weekday — no-shows, late arrivals, overtime, labor against target, sales per labor hour against the objective) and SOFT STAFFING REQUIREMENTS: a reviews diagnosis with a staffing cause ("+1 server Friday dinner", `review_requirements`) and the nightly report's open staffing actions about next week (`dsr_requirements`, until the week they concern has passed). After the draft, `applied` checks which the final rows honoured, so the review names them from the rows, not from the model's own account. Re-audit (9/29/26): a DSR ask repeated across nightly reports is one +1 with its report count; a review +1 with no daypart takes the day's busiest worked daypart (`busiest_daypart`, shift_facts) or the whole day; each review +1 is a `staff_add:` recommendation (`present_requirements`) and publishing a week that carries it records it as implemented (`record_published`, from `client_api`'s publish) — which starts the tracker on the complaint theme's share, so what_worked on the schedule and the review diagnosis can say whether the added person moved the complaints (before and after, not proof). Home's trim and the one thing's "starting with <day>" are guarded on the night the NEXT draft covers (`next_draft_date`), and the one thing starts with the heaviest day no campaign is filling. A guest text aimed at a weekday or a post tagged with an occasion or a dish is a `demand_signals` row (`record_campaign`, `record_post`), which the draft, the DSR's Tomorrow and predictions, the pre-shift and the prep list already read; what a campaign does to its night is one measurement (`demand_signals.measured_campaign_lift` → `event_memory.campaign_effect`), the same figure the forecast and the campaign's own result use. A review is placed on the daypart it names (`schedule_intel._place_reviews`) for the schedule's outcomes and calibration.

The labor read is a stored read (`insight_store`, kind `labor`) like Reviews, Food Cost and Marketing, kept in `ai_reads`; its memory, like the schedule draft's, is assembled for the team (`labor.TEAM_VIEWER` is `memory_context.TEAM`), so an owner-only line ("letting Dana go") never reaches a read a manager opens, and its target line says whose target it is. Labor history is calendar payroll weeks from final days (`models.refresh_labor_periods`), and a week-over-week change is said only between adjacent weeks on the same basis. "Over target" fires past the larger of the stated 3 points and this restaurant's own spread (`restaurant_thresholds.margin`: 1.645σ of its own history, refitted nightly — a restaurant whose labor % swings on its own is no longer flagged on noise). A cut on a day whose cuts measured worse is penalised in the optimizer (`schedule_optimizer.learned_penalty`).

## Food Cost (Inventory)

**Files**: `inventory.py` (waste/overstock/reorder), `inventory_ledger.py` (the stock ledger, recipes, menu margins, portion variance), `cogs.py` (actual food cost %), `waste_trend.py` (the trend engine), `food_cost_intelligence.py` (the CFO layer). Panel id is historically `#panel-inventory` (predates the "Food Cost" rename).

Weekly ingredient counts → waste cost, price-drift detection (this week's unit cost vs last), monthly waste projection, purchase-order drafting. CSV shape: `item,category,par_level,current_stock,unit_cost,avg_daily_usage,last_order_qty,waste_last_week`.

**The five engines and who reads them.** Four financial engines existed and the AI saw one — it narrated waste and could not say whether food cost was over target or which dish was eating the margin. `food_cost_intelligence` is the layer that joins them: it ranks the drivers, projects prime cost, and feeds all of it into both the insight prompt and the root-cause pass.

**Invariants (audit #14 — the CFO layer)**:
- `inventory_history` is written by a SCHEDULED job (`scheduler.run_food_cost_snapshots`, 05:00), not by a page render. The weekly waste series, the multi-week price trends, the price-spike alert and the opening/closing values behind food cost % all read that table — when its only writer was the AI insight on page view, all four were functions of whether the owner opened the tab, and a week nobody looked at was ABSENT from the series rather than zero in it.
- There is ONE week-over-week implementation, `waste_trend`'s ISO-week series. `week_end` is today's date, so "the previous snapshot row" is whenever the owner last looked — that was being labelled "vs last week" and a dollar forecast was built on it.
- Next week's waste is the mean of the last four ISO weeks with any outlier week left out (`forecast_log.waste_next_week`) — the old latest-week-plus-last-change momentum forecast lost to "same as last week" on pure noise (confidence audit probe W, 9/24/26). It is STORED once per ISO week (`forecast_log.record`, insert-once) and scored only after that week closes, with one error denominator (the actual). The owner is shown the bias-corrected figure when the record has a consistent lean, and nothing at all while the record reads "often wide" (`forecast_log.accuracy(...)["withheld"]`); the raw figure is always what is recorded and scored. A forecast nobody checks costs nothing to get wrong.
- `forecast_log.py` is the one place for every forecast's rules (waste, prime cost, the weekly revenue projection frozen at schedule publish, and the labor / marketing-reach / review-rating forecast lines): insert-once per period, score closed periods only (`scheduler.run_forecast_scoring`, 5am, every restaurant holding a due forecast), accuracy by kind (contract K8).
- The waste-rate denominator is what was actually received over the same seven days (`cogs.purchases_in_window`), never the sum of each item's last order — that covers a different period per item and usually a longer one than the numerator.
- Two different windows, kept apart: `week_start`/`week_end` label the WASTE period (always the trailing seven days); `counted_to`/`window_age_days`/`stock_basis` say how far the CURRENT STOCK figures can be trusted.
- Every displayed annual figure is the displayed monthly times twelve. Round once, derive the rest.
- Drivers are ranked in Python — dollars, then confidence, then ease — and the prompt is told not to re-rank them. Each carries evidence, a confidence, a difficulty and what happens if it is ignored.
- A cause is only ever stated from a stored diagnosis whose validator rejects any driver it was not given (`_validate_diagnosis`), the same discipline `verify_figures` applies to numbers.
- A projection returns nothing rather than substituting a zero, and says which component is missing. `profitability_projection` is PRIME COST, not net profit, and its basis says the labor share came from the shift analysis's own period.
- The inferred-waste gap is attributed per ingredient and per dish (`inventory_ledger.inferred_variance`) — it is over-portioning, prep loss or shrink, and aggregating it to one restaurant-wide dollar figure produced the one number an owner could not act on.

**The work, not just the read (friction audit, 9/25/26, workstream O2)**:
- A delivery goes into stock. "Received as ordered" (or "Some were short" with a quantity per line) posts each PO line with an ingredient through `inventory_ledger.record_receiving` (`client_api._do_receive_po`); the status flip claims the PO first, so a delivery can never post twice. Without this the count sheet's "expected" drifted low and the next count read the gap as unexplained waste.
- One Order card: the week's lines grouped by supplier with editable quantities, loaded when first on screen; the edits ride with the send (`inventory.apply_order_edits`) after the draft hash proves the draft unchanged, and the PO keeps the draft beside what went. Sending asks once, inline, naming the supplier, the item count and the total.
- Pending invoices live on the server (`invoices.pending_imports` — never applied, or a trusted supplier's leftovers) and list at the top of the invoice card; Home's queue item opens the invoice itself (`invoice/<id>`). An unmatched line can become an ingredient in place. Ask can apply only the lines the stored invoice marks checked (`apply_invoice_lines`) and receive the oldest open order as ordered (`receive_purchase_order`).
- Logged waste (`inventory_ledger.record_waste`, source `logged`) is counted waste; only a recount gap is `inferred`.
- Counts without the margins: `permissions.FOOD_COST_ENTER` (every console role, incl. `manager`) opens the count sheet, the PO list (money withheld), receiving and the waste log — and nothing else. A manager's web Food Cost tab is "Counts" (`fc_enter_only`), the count sheet and deliveries only.
- For a ledger account the price monitor is filled from the ingredient list, read-only until "Edit prices"; the typed tracker (`/api/food-cost-quickcount`, `food_cost_json`) is a candidate for future cleanup after additional verification — its `food_cost_data` consumers were not traced end to end.
- Recipes with every line at ≥80% support and clean units accept in one tap; the rest stay one by one.
- Each cost driver and cross-module link carries `act: {label, nav}` (`food_cost_intelligence.driver_action`, `business_intelligence.link_action`) — where the fix is done.

**What Food Cost learns from the owner (memory audit 9/29/26)**:
- Supplier orders: the owner's changes to drafted orders become a per-item factor (`ordering.order_corrections`, applied in `inventory.build_supplier_orders`) — the median of sent ÷ the drafted base quantity over the last 8 owner-sent orders, shrunk, bounded to 0.5–1.5, used from 3 orders and a 10% move. Lines carry `owner_adjusted` and `base_qty`, so a corrected draft sent unchanged confirms the factor rather than pulling it back toward 1.
- Invoices: an owner-applied line teaches `invoice_aliases`, which `invoices.propose` reads first (`matched_by`); the rule's own applies and an admin's teach nothing.
- Recipes: the owner's confirmed recipes of the same dish type go into the recipe prompt, fenced (`recipes.confirmed_examples`).
- Reprice: the suggested price still restores the previous food-cost %; beside it, the owner's own ratio to past suggestions (`menu_intelligence.reprice_acceptance`, `owner_ratio`, `typical_price`). A dish guests call poor value carries `value_note` and a lowered confidence (`lever_conflicts`), and a dish guests complain about that is also a cost driver is guarded — fix the plate before the price (`link_memory.dish_guard`; Home and the action queue skip it).
- Par: two or more 86s in 28 days suggests a higher par (`ordering.par_suggestions`; `GET /food-cost/par-suggestions`, `POST …/<id>/accept`, both surfaces). Accept writes the par and a `change_log` row and marks `raise_par:<item>` implemented; 86s from before the last raise never count again.
- The diagnosis carries purchasing (`purchasing_context`: receiving and invoice price moves on the drivers' items), what was already tried on the lead, and memory including the cross-module `links`; a diagnosis whose lead no longer ranks is retired (`retire_diagnosis`), and a stale stored read offers no controls (`controls_withheld: "stale"`). Drivers are ranked by dollars × this restaurant's measured record for that kind of fix.
- Prime cost (`profitability_projection`) sums the labor dollars the nightly report measured, and the shift analysis's share covers only the other nights; `labor_basis` / `labor_basis_text` say which, and the morning brief's stamp names it — the projection and the report's own figure differ for a stated reason, not silently.
- History outlives the prune: before `inventory_history` rows older than 395 days go, `history_rollups.summarise_inventory` writes `inventory_weekly_summary` and `ingredient_cost_monthly` (kept forever), and `waste_trend.load_waste_history` merges them, so food cost % and a seasonal re-check reach past 395 days.
- None of it learns from a restaurant that does not learn for itself (`models.learns_for_itself`), or from an admin's view-as (`reprice_decisions.authority`, `purchase_orders.authority`).

## Daily Sales Report and Reports

The nightly End-of-Day Closeout & Daily Sales Report (`dsr/`; design and
build history in `docs/plans/DSR_ENGINE_PLAN.md`). **One pipeline, one fact
snapshot, many views.** A trigger collects deterministic FACTS, block by
block, each with a status and a source. One AI narrative reads only those
facts. Every view renders from the same stored snapshot: owner, manager,
the weekly grid, email, push, the morning brief and Ask.

**The pipeline.**
- `pipeline.py` is the nightly stage machine. `run_night` handles one
  restaurant-night; `run_sweep` is the scheduler's entry, claimed per 10
  minutes; `start_manual` is the "Close day" button. A night moves
  scheduled → awaiting close → collecting → writing → final, or provisional
  when a block is still awaiting at the deadline. A night completed later is
  version 2, shown as "Updated".
- `store.py` keeps the reports (one row per restaurant, business date and
  version), their stages and facts, and the searchable `dsr_metrics`.
- `common.py` holds the business date (a 1am close belongs to the night
  before) and `local_bounds` / `utc_bounds`, because rows are stamped on two
  clocks.

**The blocks.** Each is `collect(ctx) -> block`, makes no model call, and
reports a status instead of a zero:

| Block | What it reads |
|---|---|
| `block_sales.py` | The POS night (`pos.fetch_day_sales`) against yesterday, last week, last year, the forecast and the budget; categories through the owner's map, with each department's POS item categories (`department_contents`) checked against what the department held when mapped — something new inside it is listed on its own, not counted toward the mapping (`categorize`, 10/5/26) |
| `block_labor.py` | `labor_daily_history`, overtime, the evening split, attendance; salaried pay for the owner (`access._live_salaries`); labor by department (`department_of`: the POS's payroll category, the bar and management named apart) scaled to the night's labor, and labor hours in every clock hour |
| `block_service.py` | The night's checks, read from the POS when the report runs (`common.night_archive` archives the night once per pass after Sales is in, so the figures are in version 1, not the 4am archive): meal periods, rooms, drinks per guest, servers, the loss ledger by reason and approver, punch edits, the cash-and-cards register (tenders by method, card tips, payouts and pay-ins with their approver). Never holds the night (`pipeline.NEVER_HOLDS`); a POS without check detail is quiet, not missing |
| `block_food.py` | Theoretical food cost from recipes × dishes sold, plus waste |
| `block_reviews.py` | The night's reviews and what's owed; the guest score over the trailing 7 days |
| `block_marketing.py` | What went out that night and what it did, where measured |
| `block_intel.py` | Weather (an NWS forecast, labelled so), events, competitor moves |
| `block_closeout.py` | The manager's own close-out, verbatim and attributed |

**Around the blocks.**
- `narrative.py` makes the one model call: `write(ctx, facts)` with the
  prompt from `build_prompt`. Every figure is checked against the facts
  (`PROMPT_LIBRARY.md`). Since 10/7/26 the call is one run of the
  `dsr_narrative` workflow (`run`: Sonnet 5, then Sonnet 5.5 thinking only
  on a shape `salvage` could not repair — a broken line is dropped, an
  injected field or a malformed lead is not repaired); a batch answer is the
  run's first rung (`land_batch`); an answer to a priority is the run's
  outcome (`record_action_outcome`, from `rec_ledger.record`); a later
  version whose cited facts did not move keeps the earlier narrative with no
  call (`carry_forward`, offered by `pipeline._carry_forward`). Three lines
  a list, single slots one short sentence (#37). Its memory block is the
  Restaurant Context Manager's `memory` section.
- `scorecard.py` is "Did we win today?": a verdict, an overall score out of
  100, wins and risks. Deterministic.
- `kpis.py` sets each figure beside its direction, its best or worst in
  weeks, its target and fair peers.
- `predictions.py` and `tomorrow.py` are the next night's forecast and the
  claims tomorrow's report will grade. `tomorrow.py` also prices the next
  day's published schedule against the forecast (`labor_plan`), names who
  goes past 40 hours this payroll week (`overtime_outlook`) and anyone who
  would work a 7th day in a row (`rest_day_items`).
- `kpis.pace` is the week and the period to date against the budget and last
  year, with what each night left needs (arithmetic, never a run rate);
  `kpis.big` picks the six KPIs the report leads with. The owner's labor and
  prime history add the salaried share, so all-in is compared with all-in.
- `access.py` is who sees what: one snapshot, an owner view and a manager
  view (owner-only keys such as `salaried*` and the budget).
  `access.summary` builds a list row's verdict, net and `list_stats` (the
  Reports list's figures).
- `deliver.py` sends one email and push per night, and holds pushes through
  quiet hours.
- `memory.py` covers Ask's `read_dsr` / `find_days` / `read_week` /
  `read_period`, last night in Ask's context, and the morning brief's
  "yesterday".
- `rollup.py` builds the week and the fiscal period in the shape of Erik's
  sheet, and `xlsx.py` exports it with no dependency.
- `fiscal.py` is the restaurant's fiscal calendar (week start, Period 1 /
  Week 1, a scheme of "4x13", "445", "454" or "544").
- `backfill.py` fills past nights' figures from the POS for the days before
  the report started. `history_import.py` brings Last Year in from the
  owner's old workbooks.

**Surfaces.**
- On the web, the Reports tab beside Home (`#dsr/list`): every night newest
  first, each with its verdict, net and a strip of figures (vs last week,
  vs forecast, labor %, guests, average check, overtime). Night, Week and

- On iOS, the Daily Report.
- Routes are in `API_REFERENCE.md` → *The nightly DSR*.

## Ask Cavnar

**Files**: `ask_cavnar.py` (context snapshot builder + `ask_with_tools`), `ask_cavnar_tools.py` (the tool registry — `len(TOOLS)` is the count), `business_intelligence.py` (the cross-module layer), `home_brief.py` (feeds the opening briefing), `ask_conversations.py` (the rolling chat summary, past chats, what the last answer read), `owner_memory.py` (what the owner tells Cavnar AI, typed).

**Design stance**: an AI-powered restaurant COO, not a chatbot wrapper. Every question gets a fresh `build_context()` snapshot (identity, sibling locations, alerts, memory, module data the restaurant's tier actually has) plus a filtered tool list (`tool_specs(restaurant)` — a tool tagged with a module the restaurant doesn't have is never offered, so the model can't call it and produce an empty-result apology).

**On the orchestrator** (AI orchestration design, 10/7/26): each question is one `ask_cavnar` run (T2, no escalation) whose id is the turn's `ask:` correlation id; a rating of the answer is its outcome (`record_feedback_outcome`). The snapshot's memory and, for an owner-level asker with no module denied, its ACROSS THE BUSINESS block come from `restaurant_context` sections (a manager's view is built for the manager, as before); the sections' fixed reading rules sit in the cached static block (`_SNAPSHOT_RULES`, #64); a follow-up in the same chat within ten minutes is handed the last answer's reads as its first tool results (`ask_conversations.remember_reads` / `replay_reads`, #65); a confirmation card raised beside a written answer skips the forced second call (#67). See PROMPT_LIBRARY.md → *The DSR narrative and Ask on the orchestrator*.

**Screen context** (friction #15, 9/25/26): every question may carry `screen` {panel, entity}; `ask_cavnar.screen_hint` turns it into one server-built line (a review by id from this restaurant, described by rating, platform, date and reply status only), never an instruction — see PROMPT_LIBRARY.md → Ask. The web sends it with every question, and a review card's or an Intel recommendation's "Ask about this" sets the item; the chat id is kept per restaurant in sessionStorage so the panel reopens on it after a reload or a location switch.

**Role scoping**: Ask answers as the asker, not as the restaurant. Every route (web/mobile, stream/non-stream) passes `current_user`; `ask_with_tools(..., user=)` swaps in `ask_cavnar_tools.viewer_restaurant(restaurant, user)` — a copy with every module the role can't read (`permissions.MODULE_VIEW_PERMISSIONS`) switched off — so the snapshot, the cross-module money, the offered tools and every tool call leave it out. `run_read_tool` re-checks `tool_allowed` (the offered list is not the permission check); food-cost metrics in goals/outcomes are filtered via `metric_visible`. The context cache is keyed by (restaurant, denied modules). Home (`home_brief._build`) and the Ask opening built from it filter the same way.

**Roles on a team**: an owner invites or re-roles a login as Co-owner (stored `client` — every owner right), Manager, or Teammate (`member`) — `auth.TEAM_ROLES`, `auth.set_team_role` (writes users.role and the membership together; never your own role; never the last owner — revoke refuses that too). Owner mail that addresses "the owner" goes to every owner: `scheduler.get_owner_emails` (weekly digest); the morning brief already goes to each recipient.

**Owner-granted access**: an owner can open Food cost & margins (`FOOD_COST_VIEW`) and, separately, Comps & voids (`LOSS_VIEW`) to an individual manager at one location — Account → Team, web and iOS. Stored in `permission_grants`, loaded per request by `auth.get_session_user` into `current_user["grants"]`, honoured by `permissions.has_permission` for `GRANTABLE` permissions on `GRANTABLE_ROLES` only. Because every gate goes through `has_permission`, a grant opens the routes, the dashboard tab, Home, Ask and the brief at once; revoking takes effect on the next request.

**Tool kinds** (`TOOLS` registry, each entry `{kind, fn, module, spec}`):
- `read` — executes immediately, returns data (e.g. `read_schedule`, `read_team`, `read_alerts`; since the memory round `read_past_conversations` (this login's earlier chats), `read_recent_reads` (what Cavnar AI already told this owner elsewhere, dated, scoped by each surface's permission — `ai_reads.SURFACE_MODULE` / `OWNER_ONLY`), `read_upcoming` (what is on the books by date), `read_forecast_record` (how each forecast has held up here), `read_closeouts` (the closers' own notes), `read_target_history` (which labor, food cost, waste and revenue targets applied on a date, and every change to them — a target only for a login that may see its module), `read_service_performance` (each server's measured record — sales per cover, check average, drinks per entrée, card tip rate, table turn, comps — plus rooms, dayparts and kitchen speed where the POS records it, from the POS archive; account holders only), `read_market_history` (competitors opening, closing and moving nearby and the own rating, Intel view) and `read_marketing_results` (post lift and each guest text's return, with its target night's measured effect)). `read_dsr` carries the report's Tomorrow and how the previous night's predictions graded; `read_competitors` returns only the open Intel recommendations (answered and withheld lines out).
- `action` — executes immediately, no confirmation (reserved for low-stakes/reversible calls): `remember` / `forget` (viewer-aware — a fact is written with its author and audience, and refused once the turn has read public text), `edit_review_reply`.
- `write` — returns a **proposal** (`build_proposal`) the client renders as a confirm card; the actual route it posts to on confirm is the same authenticated endpoint a manual button already uses, so a proposal can never reach anything the owner couldn't do themselves. `decide_time_off` / `decide_shift_request` (Friction #18, with the read `read_time_off`) put the request id in the path and name the person from the stored PENDING request, never the model's words; a request that is answered or not this restaurant's builds no card. `add_closed_date` and `set_staff_unavailable` (memory audit 9/29/26) are write tools for exactly the facts `remember` used to hold as prose: a closure the schedule and every forecast obey, and a weekday a person on the roster cannot work (their other blocked days kept). `set_staff_hours` (schedule audit 10/3/26 PR-19) records part of a day — a time they must be done by or can start from, or lunch/day or dinner/night only — merged into the person's stored windows and daypart availability through the Team screen's staff-settings route: "can't close Sundays" is no longer a whole Sunday blocked. `generate_schedule` carries the week, the days of its draft to rewrite (the card adds the draft's id) and the owner's own words for the draft, which reach every model call of the generation.

**The opening** (`/mobile/api/ask-cavnar/opening`, delegated on web): built from `home_brief`'s attention items + one win, **no model call** — instant, and can't hallucinate since nothing here is generated. The only cache behind it is `home_brief`'s 60-second per-restaurant memo, so a fixed issue stops being raised within a minute.

**Memory** (`owner_memory`, stored in `ask_memory`): the model calls `remember`/`forget` deliberately; nothing lands automatically. A fact is typed (constraint, context, preference, goal, follow-up — one lane each, split by whose it is so no one else's notes displace the owner's, the least-used evicted first into `ask_memory_archive`, a pinned one never), keyed by its text AND its author (another login's identical sentence is a confirmation or its own row, never a takeover), refused when it is a measurable target in any kind, and handed back with the notes it may replace; it carries who said it, who may read it (the team, the account holders, only its author) and how long it holds, and names the modules it is about — so it reaches the schedule draft, the labor and food reads, the nightly report, marketing and review replies through `memory_context`, not only Ask. Ask's own snapshot reads it the same way (surface `ask`, `ASK_MEMORY_BUDGET_CHARS` = 4,000). The section says these are the owner's and the team's words, not measured data, so the model never presents a stated goal as something it computed; a goal set in Goals is the target modules judge against (`owner_memory.target_for`). A suggestion the owner already declined on any surface is caveated in the prose too ("you passed on this on M/D/YY", `decisions.annotate_declined`, `meta.declined_repeats`).

**Conversations** (`ask_conversations`, memory audit 9/29/26): Ask replays the last 12 messages of a chat, and the last answer in full (up to 16,000 characters) so "do the second one" resolves against it; older answers replay cut to 800 characters (AI cost audit 10/7/26). Once turns scroll out of that window, one small validated model call (`ask_summary`, Haiku) folds them into a rolling summary — decisions, figures read, proposals, open questions, the owner's aim — replayed as a per-turn context block after the snapshot in every later turn, fenced as model-written (memory_context surface `ask_conversation`). Each turn keeps its tool calls, so the next is told what the last answer read. `read_past_conversations` searches this login's earlier chats and `ask_topics` (where an evicted chat's title, summary and topics are kept), and a "questions you often ask" line says what this person keeps coming back to. Chats are kept 50 per login and 250 per restaurant, a manager's daily chats no longer pushing out the owner's.

**Across modules** (`business_intelligence.py`, audit #15): the layer that answers "why did profits drop" rather than six single-module answers. `gather()` collects each module's own executive brief; `correlations()` reports where two of them point at the same day, dish or shift; `money_at_stake()` ranks each module's monthly dollar figure against the others. Links are no longer forgotten between calls (memory audit 9/29/26): every link a read finds is kept in `link_memory` (`bi_links`: first and last found, weeks running, how it was resolved — done or implemented from the ledger, declined and never re-surfaced, or gone when a read of both modules no longer finds it, `settle` nightly), a link found three weeks running or back after an answer is `recurring` and ranks higher in the one-thing pick (`RECURRING_LINK_WEIGHT`, never critical), and each kind reaches the decision it was missing — the food diagnosis reads the food links, the reprice guard and the Opportunity Feed read `reviews_x_menu`, the marketing generators the do-not-promote list and their own links. Two kinds were added: `dsr_x_reviews` (complaints on the weekday the nightly report keeps logging no-shows) and `marketing_x_labor` (a fill-a-night campaign on the day labor runs heaviest). Re-audit (9/29/26): every reader of the brief's links reads only the unanswered ones (`unanswered_links` inside `executive_brief` — link memory, the ledger's silences including the login's own, declined signatures), before the one-thing cap; Ask gets answered ones only through `read_restaurant_memory`'s link history, labelled; a Done link found again lifts its Done silence; the nightly `learning_memory` pass observes links, and a module whose data lapsed is not "consulted"; a marketing x labor link ends once its night has passed, saying what the night measured (`resolved_by` measured / window_closed); a reviews x menu link names one menu row; a reviews x labor link states the guests per labor hour the nightly reports measured instead of asking the owner to check covers; staffing advice signatures carry their direction (`:hold` / `:add`). It invents no thresholds — a link only exists when both sides already cleared their own module's evidence floor — and it never states a co-occurrence as a cause: each link carries `confirm_by` and `alternative`, and the labour link carries `not_a_cause` because this product has no service-time or cover-count data. The money lines are deliberately never summed (a measured cost, a scheduling gap and an elasticity forecast are not addends), and a range stays a range. Reached by the model through `read_business_snapshot`, and summarised into every snapshot by `snapshot_block()`.

**Grounding**: `ai_guard.verify_figures` runs on every answer against the snapshot + every tool payload + the replayed history. An untraceable figure caps confidence at `low` and reaches both clients, which keep the answer and caveat the number. The answer's Historical Accuracy comes from how answers on the same topic were rated here (`rating_record`, from `RATED_MIN` ratings; the topic is `ask_conversations.answer_topic`).

**Depth** (`_depth_for`): `brief` (Home box, 3 sentences), `standard`, `executive` (what/why/evidence/dollars/action/confidence/what-to-watch). Chosen from the question, deterministically — except that a login whose own ratings say long answers have not helped them (`owner_memory.rating_preference` "short") gets `standard` for an executive question, and the turn carries an answer-length note either way.

**Prompt caching**: `system` is a list of blocks — static rules with a `cache_control` breakpoint, then the live snapshot. ~9,700 tokens cached per turn. Never interpolate per-restaurant data into the static block.

**The nightly DSR** (`dsr/memory.py`): Ask's snapshot carries last night's report (headline figures by fact key, what was missing, the narrative's lead and actions) and four viewer-aware read tools — `read_dsr`, `find_days` (SQL over `dsr_metrics`), `read_week`, `read_period`. Every read goes through `dsr.access` for the asker (`viewer_restaurant` stamps `_ask_dsr_user`), so a manager never reads the budget, prime cost, comps/voids or food cost here either; `find_days` refuses those metrics outright, and the context cache key carries the DSR view.

**Don't touch casually**: the module-gating in `tool_specs()`, and the read/write split — a tool that reaches an outside effect (email, public post, scheduled deletion) must be `write`, never `read`/`action`. `auto_approve` and `data_retention` are write tools for exactly this reason. And never call `client_api._do_ai_visibility` from the context path: on a cache miss it fires live Perplexity queries.

## The owner's day (workflow audit #19)

**Files**: `closeout.py`, `action_queue.py`, `command_center.py`, `nav.py`, `intraday.py`, `monthly_review.py`, `weekly_review.py`, plus the day-shaped parts of `notify.py`, `morning_brief.py`, `issues.py` and `strategy_jobs.py`.

- **Before opening** — the brief is the one morning surface. A night counts as watched only when the check read its clock-ins (`schedule_intel.watched_dates`, L-32). A gap whose shift has ended with no clock-in stops asking for a cover — the issue says "didn't come in" and the ask is refused — and an issue still open from an earlier business date closes itself with that note, which the nightly attendance read still records as a no-show (`issues.close_ended_coverage`, run by every check pass, open hours or not; 10/5/26: Home showed a 3:30pm shift's "Ask … to cover" at 4:20pm, and a day's issues never left Home).

The night's report itself is the **Daily report** (`dsr/`): Home's "Last night" card opens it at `#dsr/YYYY-MM-DD` — for the owner it opens with **Today's score** (`dsr/scorecard.py`: the verdict, the overall score out of 100, sales vs budget, labor vs target, food cost, guest experience), then the **executive summary**, then **Today's wins** and **Today's risks** (measured signals first, topped up from the narrative's verified lines) — the same order in the email, the push and the iPhone report; the manager's report is operations, not finance — its own **operations summary**, **Today's shift** (employees scheduled, no-shows, late arrivals, overtime, shift quality; break compliance joins when Cavnar has a source) and **Operations** (average ticket, guests, complaints, sales per labor hour; comps and voids — and gross, which would reveal them — only when the owner grants them); its Top KPIs never repeat an Operations tile. Both reports then carry the **key numbers with direction** (`dsr/kpis.py`: vs the same weekday, best/worst in weeks, a trend line, the target, restaurants like yours only when fair), **AI insights** (the narrative is told what its previous actions became — YESTERDAY'S PRIORITIES with their ledger state — and how its predictions graded, and reads memory assembled for the team, since one narrative renders into both views), **Tomorrow's priorities** (up to five actions), **Tomorrow** (`dsr/tomorrow.py`: time off, weather, events, low stock, Cavnar's forecast and an AI confidence % — only the forecast's measured record of how often its range held, "—" until there is one), the blocks, and **How did yesterday turn out?** (`dsr/predictions.py`: yesterday's predictions — each written before its night opened — graded, with the running accuracy; the budget prediction is the owner's; a rain or event prediction is made only in the direction this restaurant has measured, `event_memory`); tomorrow's actions (answered on rec_ledger's `dsr` surface), every block with its source or the reason it isn't ready, the close-out verbatim — with Close day (and, for the owner, re-run any of the last seven nights), the weekly grid in the owner's own week with an .xlsx export, and the period.
- **After close** — `closeout.py` is the manager's handoff (an 86 names an ingredient by whole words, and only what a filing changed is acted on — a re-save does not re-zero the item; F2-8): four quick lines plus the six fields the nightly DSR reads (equipment, VIP guests, maintenance, shift notes, general notes, and "why the day went how it did"). ## Strategic foundations (audit #18)

**Files**: `metrics.py`, `outcomes.py`, `goals.py`, `menu_intelligence.py`, `demand.py`, `loss_detection.py`, `issues.py`, `invoices.py`, `morning_brief.py`, `preshift.py`, `strategy_routes.py` (HTTP, web + mobile twins + the public `/i/<token>` issue page), `strategy_jobs.py` (scheduled half).

**Design stance**: every number is measured, never generated. `metrics.py` is the one registry of "what can be measured before and after" (unknown is `None`, never 0; each metric has a noise band below which the verdict is `no_clear_change`). `outcomes.py` tracks a committed change against that metric and always carries `CAUSATION_CAVEAT` — before/after, not proven cause. `goals.py` holds one active target per metric; since the memory round a goal is the target a module judges against (`owner_memory.target_for`, read first by `thresholds.target_for`), a delegate's goal is `proposed` until an account holder confirms or declines it, and setting, confirming or ending one (`end_goal(…, user_id=, authority=)`) writes a `change_log` row.

**Outcomes after the rec-ROI audit** (`outcomes.py`, `metrics.py`; contracts in API_REFERENCE.md → "Outcomes and value"):
- **Metrics**: fourteen — `labor_pct`, `overtime_hours` (hours past 40 per whole payroll week from the shifts file; a week with no shifts is unknown; dollars at the overtime premium only, `metrics.overtime_premium_per_hour` = blended wage × (1.5 − 1)), `food_cost_pct`, `weekly_waste`, `sales`, `weekday_sales:<Day>`, `avg_rating`, `complaints:<cat>`, `response_hours` (median review-to-reply hours, ≥5 replies, no dollars), `comp_rate`, `void_rate`, and the finer grains (memory audit 9/29/26) `labor_pct_day:<Day>` (one weekday's labor %), `labor_pct_part` (one weekday's morning or night — only on days whose sales split was measured intraday, never the assumed 40/60) and `item_waste:<item>`, so trackers on Tuesday and on Friday run side by side (`metrics.grain`, `slices_collide`). `metrics.FAMILIES` groups the ones that measure the same money or experience (labor cost, food cost, sales, guest rating, reply speed, comps, voids). `compare` returns the `band` it used and the `multiple` of it the move covered; a relative band has a floor (`_NOISE_FLOOR`).
- **One tracker per number.** `outcomes.record(gate=)` refuses a second tracker on the same metric (`"metric"`, an owner-pressed Track and Ask) or anywhere in the family (`"family"`, every automatic start — `AUTOMATIC_SOURCES`: observed actions, reprice, campaigns, schedule accepts — and Accept/Done on `recs/event`, Home Done; re-audit A18). Metric keys are normalised (`metrics.normalize`: `weekday_sales:tuesday` is `weekday_sales:Tuesday`). The gate is checked again inside `BEGIN IMMEDIATE`, so two starts at once are one tracker, never a 500. `observe()` records at most one tracker per metric per calendar month, whatever became of the first. `outcomes.start` turns a refusal (`TrackerRefused`) into the `tracker_refused` reply. An alert-opened tracker is informational and never blocks.
- **Dates** are the restaurant's own (`outcomes.local_today`), for starts, evaluations, re-checks, accrual and campaign keys; the evaluation job runs one restaurant at a time, bounded and resumable (`strategy_jobs._bounded_each`).
- **What a recommendation measures**: `outcomes.metric_for_rec` — a DSR action by its kind (`DSR_ACTION_METRICS`: control_hours/adjust_staffing → labor %, reduce_waste → waste, respond_reviews → reply time, the rest nothing — authoritative), a kind that names its own number (`slow_day:<Day>` → that weekday's sales; `overtime` / `overtime_move:…` → `overtime_hours`; authoritative whatever surface showed it), else the metric it was presented with (`rec_instances.expected_metric`). With a `viewer`, a metric the login may not see is never started from its answer (`metric_visible_to`: food cost needs FOOD_COST_VIEW, comps/voids LOSS_VIEW). Track falls back to the module's natural metric (`strategy_routes.REC_TRACK_METRICS`); Marketing and Intel have none.
- **Linked to its recommendation** (memory audit 9/29/26): `outcomes.record` / `start(rec_key=)` store the recommendation a tracker measures (`recommendation_outcomes.measures_key`), which sets the module credit, the trigger window (the recommendation's own episode) and the episode linked at start; `rec_ledger._sync_trackers` carries the verdict to every linked episode. The campaign tracker passes the feed card or `slow_day:<Day>`, a reprice every `reprice:<Dish>` implemented that month, Ask's track (`ask_cavnar_tools.track_rec_key`) the key the model names when that episode exists, else the one recent episode on the metric — two candidates link none. A change marked implemented starts its tracker (`autostart_implemented`; the nightly learning pass catches up with `autostart_due`), and `find_concurrent` also lists price, menu, roster, pay, hours and supplier changes from `change_log`.
- **Credit**: `module` is stored on the row from the recommendation (`resolve_module`: the kind's own (`KIND_MODULE`: a slow day is Marketing) → `rec_instances.module` → DSR block → the caller's → source → the metric's `METRIC_MODULE`, where `sales` is `other`). The caller's module comes after the recommendation's: iOS sends the screen a Track was pressed on.
- **Readings** (`outcomes._measure`): a window counted in trading days (labor %, sales, a weekday's sales, comps, voids) is unknown unless ≥ `MIN_COVERAGE` (70%) of its trading days were measured (`metrics.coverage`: the weekdays traded over the 8 weeks ending with the window, less the owner's stated closures) — baseline, after-window, re-check and each accrual day alike. Comps and voids are read over the days the POS was asked (every asked day has a row, zero included). Waste is priced only when ≥90% of its events carry a unit cost. A move of zero is never a move.
- **Baselines** (`_baseline`): `prior window` for metrics a season does not move; `matched weekdays` for labor %, food cost %, sales (a window a whole number of weeks back); `same weeks last year` when ≥80% of the trading days of both of last year's windows are measured — this year's before-window moved as those weeks moved last year, compared with a band widened by `SEASONAL_BAND_SCALE`. Weekday-sensitive trackers (`metrics.WEEKDAY_MIX_METRICS`) run whole weeks; a legacy window of another length is re-read a whole number of weeks from its start (`_aligned_window`).
- **Evaluation** stores after-value, delta, `delta_pct`, the other changes in the window (`find_concurrent`: trackers and accepted recommendations on the same family, never one the owner disowned; for a labor/food-cost/comp/void share, a sales move past its band (`sales_move`) and any sales tracker; price changes (not the reprice tracker's own), owner events, unbalanced holidays — by NAME against last year for a `same weeks last year` baseline — and closures for trading-volume families), a `trend` entry when the baseline's two halves show the number already moving that way by enough to explain the move (`pre_trend`), and the grade (`grade`, the one rule evaluate, recheck and apply_checkin all use, with the owner's check-in): `none` / `associated` (past the band once, or anything with a concurrent change, an unchecked window, or a check-in saying conditions changed or "no") / `consistent` (≥ `CONSISTENT_MULTIPLE` bands, nothing else changing) / `held` (still past the band at re-check, nothing else changing). No label claims cause.
- **Calibration (confidence audit 9/24/26; INTELLIGENCE_ENGINE.md → "What a measured result is allowed to say")**: a triggered tracker's baseline is the mirror of its after-window about the window that fired the recommendation (`trigger_window_for`, `mirror_window`, `baseline_kind` "before the trigger"), never that window; one that could only be read against it is `baseline_overlaps_trigger` — shown, never counted. Every comparison uses the restaurant's own noise band stored on the tracker (`metrics.noise_band`, `outcomes._cmp`; the stated band is its floor; `false_alarm_rate` stated). `outcomes.result_counts` is the one admission rule value and learning share (the learning == value test): a "something else changed" check-in now stops Delivered and accrual like a "no"; a supplier order is informational. `total_value` / `delivered` / `cumulative.by_grade` report `consistent_monthly` (clear or held) apart from `associated_monthly`; `summarise` and Ask's decision lines carry the grade ("held" only when held). `observe_untaken` measures advice shown and not taken, informationally, as the comparison group (`rec_learning.untaken_comparison`). `goals` "met" needs the reading past the target by the band (else `at_target`). Schedule night recommendations are judged on ≥7 watched nights against the 12 weeks before (`schedule_intel.night_rate_verdict`); a daypart nobody watched has `issues: null` and an `issues_label`.
- **Re-check** (`recheck`, daily job): at `recheck_on` = max(start + 90, evaluate_on + window) the window ending the day before is read against the same baseline — `held` / `faded` / `reversed` / `unknown`. Faded or reversed stops counting and stops accruing. `validated` = improved + held + nothing else on the family + not disowned.
- **Pricing** (`metrics.monthly_dollars(window=)`): a share of sales (labor %, food cost %, comps, voids) and sales itself are priced on the after-window's sales per trading day × the restaurant's trading days a month (`days_per_month`: weekdays traded × 52/12 — 26 for a restaurant closed Mondays), and accrue per trading day on days actually traded and measured (`accrual_days`).
- **Value**: `total_value` keeps one reading per family per overlapping window (`distinct`): a narrower metric overlapping a broader one is the broader one's (overtime inside labor %, waste inside food cost %; `metrics.BREADTH`) — wins and losses netted — and of the rest the non-overlapping set of largest dollars (weighted interval scheduling). A labor/food-cost/comp/void move over the same weeks as a sales move the same way is set aside (`_drop_sales_artifacts`). `monthly` is the SAVINGS that improved, `worsened` `{count, monthly, priced_count}` beside it, `net_monthly` the difference (may be negative, `net_note` says so), `validated_monthly` the part that held; `annual` is a labelled projection. A sales rise is gross revenue and is reported apart in `sales_lift`, never added to the savings (`sales_pricing: "separate"`). Rating and reply-time wins count in `wins_measured` / `wins_by_module` with a `result_line`, never priced. `cumulative` sums `outcome_value_days` in SQL: the after-window's measured days at the evaluated figure's per-day share, then one trailing re-read a day while the move holds (capped at the evaluated figure, up to `ACCRUAL_HORIZON_DAYS`), net of losses — one reading per family and day (the broadest that held, then the largest), disowned trackers out, a cost day on a day sales moved the same way out, sales days summed apart in `cumulative.sales_lift`.
- **Viewers** (`outcomes.visible_to`, `value_delivered.viewer_scope`): a tracker is a login's to see when its metric is and the recommendation behind it is (`rec_learning.viewer_sees` on the linked episode). `/outcomes`, `/value`, the Home value block, the owner report and the win push all drop the rest before anything is summed; abandon leaves an unseen tracker alone with the same `{ok: true}`.
- **Menu**: `menu_intelligence.dish_scorecard` joins margin (inventory_ledger) to review dish mentions; `reprice_suggestions` refuses sample inventory and only proposes a price that restores the previous food-cost %.
- **Demand**: `demand.py` forecasts from medians of the same weekday; slow days and a prep list follow from it.
- **Loss**: `loss_detection.py` — comps/voids/refunds from POSes that report them (RPOWER); a spike needs 2× baseline AND $75 AND 4 events; approver concentration attributes to the APPROVING manager. ## Customer value / ROI (audit #20)

**Files**: `value_delivered.py` (the four figures, and `rates()` — every
stated rate in one object the payload carries), `outcomes.py`
(`total_value`, `cumulative`, `best_ever`, `realised`, `module_of_row`), `promise.py` (the
sales audit, measured), `metrics.py` (`comp_rate`/`void_rate`),
`models.money_surfaced`, `owner_report.py` ("What worked for you" —
`what_worked(rid, days, viewer)` behind `GET /recs/what-worked`, Home's card
under the worth section, the Recommendations page and the owner's monthly
email).

**Design stance**: the product must be able to say what it has been worth
without saying anything it cannot defend line by line.

- **Four figures, never summed.** `delivered` is measured before/after from
  outcomes and carries `CAUSATION_CAVEAT`. `avoided` is cost avoidance with
  every rate stated in the payload. `surfaced` is what the alerts carried in
  dollars. `opportunity` is the gap against target — money available, not
  banked. Adding any two of them mixes a measurement with an estimate.
- **The denominator travels with the total.** Every surface that prints
  "$X across N changes" also prints how many finished trackers came back
  unmeasurable or flat.
- **Net, measured, and sales apart (re-audit A6, A29, A36).** Every surface
  that prints delivered carries what got worse beside it: `headline()` and
  `home_block()` give both Homes `net_monthly`, `worsened` and `cumulative`
  (contract K4); the value snapshots and `compute_total_value_delivered` are
  the net; the monthly and lifecycle emails say it through
  `value_delivered.value_lines` — net, the ×12 figure called a projection,
  the sum over measured days, and a sales lift as gross revenue kept apart;
  milestones cross their tiers on dollars actually measured
  (`outcomes.cumulative`), never a run-rate × 12. `headline()` is light: no
  best-ever, no rates.
- **A recommendation is trackable when it names a metric.** `home_brief`'s
  `add_rec(metric=)` is what puts a "Track this" control on the card; four
  of the nine carry `None` because no honest metric fits, and they render
  without one.
- **Count work that happened, not time that passed.** The marketing figure
  was `months_since_signup × $1,500` off one post; schedules were counted
  per generated DRAFT (5,672 of them for one restaurant). Distinct months
  with content, distinct weeks scheduled.
- **`promise.compare`** puts the sales audit's own range beside where the
  metric stood then and stands now, and stops. It does not score itself, and
  a category with no metric in this product (bar, waitlist, marketing
  revenue) says so rather than being dropped or zeroed.

- **"What worked for you" is sentences over stored rows, never a model.**
  `owner_report` joins the ledger summary (acceptance with ignored in the
  denominator), the stored before/after results of recommendations the
  owner took (one per metric per non-overlapping window, no-clear-change
  included so the average is not only the movers), the measured days over
  the window (net) and the most effective tag. Each clause has its own
  minimum and is left out below it; the words are "associated with" and
  "measured", never "caused" or "saved you". The web shows it on Home and on
  the Recommendations page (`#recs` — what you followed by area, the
  check-in, and every recommendation with its answer, reason and result).

**Don't touch casually**: the separation of the four figures, and
`metrics.DAYS_PER_MONTH`/`WEEKS_PER_MONTH` — one calendar, derived from
52/12, matching `inventory.WEEKS_PER_MONTH`.

## Memory and learning (memory audit 9/29/26)

**Files**: `memory_context.py` (the assembler), `owner_memory.py`, `ask_conversations.py`, `ai_reads.py`, `learning_memory.py`, `restaurant_thresholds.py`, `learning_scorecard.py`, `link_memory.py`, `event_memory.py`, `canonical_facts.py`, `change_log.py`, `history_rollups.py`, `preferences.py`, `policy_notice.py`; `models.learning_eligible` (pooled) and `models.learns_for_itself` (own).

**Design stance**: every prompt builder used to pick its own memory by hand with fixed caps, so only Ask and the schedule prompt saw any of it, and most of what the product learned was overwritten, pruned or never read. Now what the owner said, what Cavnar AI said before, what the owner answered and what was measured are kept, reach every model call on the subject, and are read by the same rules everywhere.

**The assembler** (`memory_context.memory_context(rid, surface, viewer=, subjects=)`): a builder asks once and gets fenced, M/D/YY-dated sections in priority order within a budget (`block.text`, empty when there is nothing to say). Sections come from `PROVIDERS`, a name → `"module:function"` registry imported at call time, each provider living in the module that owns the fact:

| Section | Provider | What it carries |
|---|---|---|
| `owner_rules` | `owner_memory.rule_lines` | an account holder's own constraints and preferences, as rules to follow (the OWNER_RULE fence), taken first up to a count floor |
| `constraints` | `owner_memory.constraint_lines` | what the team told Cavnar AI, and the account holders' context, for the modules the surface plans for |
| `goals` | `owner_memory.goal_lines` | active goals on the metrics the surface judges (`SURFACE_METRICS`) |
| `last_claim` | `ai_reads.claim_lines` | the last read on the subject, what was answered and what the number did since (before and after, never proof) |
| `decisions` | `decisions.memory_lines` | the owner's answers, relevance-ranked, reasoned declines first |
| `what_worked` | `rec_learning.what_worked_lines` | this restaurant's measured record by kind and tag |
| `links` | `link_memory.link_lines` | the cross-module links kept, and the do-not-promote list |
| `events` | `event_memory.memory_lines` | measured effects of events, holidays, weather and campaigns for the dates in play |
| `market` | `event_memory.market_lines` | the public history: competitors that opened, closed or moved nearby in the last 180 days and the restaurant's own Google rating change (Intel-gated, names fenced; re-audit 9/29/26, INVENTORY-6) — competitor read, review read, weekly plan, marketing, Ask |
| `people` | `people.memory_lines` | attendance, roles, covers, standing patterns, staffing changes, guest mentions |
| `schedule_memory` | `schedule_memory.memory_lines` | what the scheduling has learned — habits, openers, sections, teams, overtime, late closes — with how sure; the labor read and Ask only (schedule audit 10/3/26 L-29) |
| `marketing` | `marketing.memory_lines` | post verdicts, returns by audience, voice, the review-request conversion |
| `conversation` | `owner_memory.conversation_lines` → `ask_conversations.memory_lines` | the chat's rolling summary, what the last answer read, the questions this login often asks |

`SURFACE_SECTIONS` names which sections each surface reads — `schedule`, `labor_read`, `review_read`, `review_diagnosis`, `reply_drafter`, `food_read`, `food_diagnosis`, `marketing`, `marketing_read`, `competitor_read`, `dsr_narrative`, `weekly_plan`, `brief`, `digest`, `ask`, `ask_conversation`. `ask` leaves out decisions and what worked (Ask's own snapshot carries them), and `marketing` is on no marketing or reply surface (those generators build richer blocks themselves). Within a section a line about a subject in play (an advice signature, a ledger tag, `conversation:<id>`) ranks first, then its weight, then recency, so a relevant old decline displaces an irrelevant recent one. The owner's rules are taken first, up to `RULE_FLOOR_COUNT` lines whatever they cost; then `DEFAULT_BUDGET_CHARS` (2,400; Ask's snapshot passes 4,000) is shared by `SECTION_SHARES`, and what is left goes back in priority order to sections that dropped lines; a section that cut the owner's or the team's words says how many more are on file. A line and its `measured` suffix (a claim's outcome, an event's lift) are one unit. `now` is the restaurant's local time, and stored UTC stamps are dated by its day. Section sizes, drops, cut calls and provider errors are logged per call and kept in `ai_memory_sizes` (the admin AI page's "Memory in prompts"). A provider that is not in the codebase is skipped; one that raises is recorded on the block, logged and captured, never raised into the model call (memory re-audit 9/29/26).

**Who reads what.** A line carries an `audience` (team, principals, author) and a `module`, and a line the viewer may not read never reaches the prompt. A surface whose output more than one login reads — a stored read, a diagnosis, the schedule draft, a public reply or post, the nightly narrative, the weekly plan (`SHARED_SURFACES`) — is always assembled for the team (`memory_context.TEAM`, `team_viewer(surface)`: a manager's view with no grants, plus the surface's own module only when every reader of that output holds it — Food Cost for the food read and diagnosis and the weekly plan), whatever viewer the caller passes: owner-only lines and comps and voids never reach it. An output with no login behind it (the weekly digest, an unattended Ask) is read as `memory_context.PRINCIPALS` — the account holders' view, never one login's own line; a line about several modules needs every one's view, and a comp or void goal needs the loss view (memory re-audit, 9/29/26). Personnel and money default to owners only whoever says them (`owner_memory.is_private`), with a one-tap "Share with the team" in Account. A new surface must be classified (`tests/test_mem_int_privacy.py`). Every line a person or a model wrote is fenced (`ai_guard.wrap_untrusted`), with who said it and until when inside the fence beside the words; an account holder's own constraint or preference is fenced as an owner rule (`ai_guard.wrap_owner_rule`), which the prompt says to follow, and neither fence is ever a source of figures; what Cavnar AI measured is trusted.

**What Cavnar AI said, kept and scored** (`ai_reads`): every read that was written — a module read, a diagnosis, the nightly narrative, a brief, a digest, the monthly review, the competitor read — is one append-only row (the shown and raw text, the verdict, a hash of its facts, the ids it cited, the recommendation keys it carried); a new read supersedes the old, and `insight_store` and the diagnosis tables stay the current pointers the screens read. The checkable claims inside them (`ai_claims`: a diagnosis's cause and the outcome it expected, a nightly action) are one open claim per surface and subject, scored at their horizon against the same number of days before with this restaurant's own noise band — "untested" when the advice was never taken. Raw reads and claims are kept 400 days (`ops._RETENTION_DAYS`), each closed quarter summarised first (`ai_read_summaries`, forever). Read back by Ask (`read_recent_reads`), the next prompt on the subject (`last_claim`), and Historical Accuracy for the diagnosis kinds.

**The nightly learning pass** (`learning_memory.nightly`, run by `scheduler.run_learning_memory` at 6am CT over every restaurant in service that may teach a learner, bounded and resumable, each step isolated): score claims whose horizon passed; write the quarterly summaries; this month's what-worked record (`rec_learning.snapshot_what_worked`); start the tracker of every change made without one (`outcomes.autostart_due`); refit the trigger margins (`restaurant_thresholds.refresh` — each the larger of the stated margin and 1.645σ of this restaurant's own history, stored with value, stated, σ, k, n, as-of and version; the labor-over margins today); this month's learning scorecard (`learning_scorecard`: reply edit rate, acceptance, measured success, forecast error by kind, draft acceptance, Ask's helpful rate and fatigue, one row per month kept forever; a worsening or flat curve is an admin-console flag, and while 75% of at least 20 settled recommendations were dismissed or ignored, Home, the nightly report's actions and the feed show fewer); and settle the cross-module links.

**What each night teaches** (`event_memory`): once a night is final (the DSR pipeline's hook, and the 5am `event_memory` job that re-reads the last week, captures observed weather and backfills history), each thing flagged on it — a listed event, a holiday, the closer's influence note, observed rain, a guest-text campaign, payday — is a row with the night's lift against the same weekday's median on one net basis. Per label, `measured_effect` says whether it `applies` (three nights, or a holiday's one night past its threshold) and nothing acts on it before then. The forecast (`demand.forecast_day`), `demand_signals.by_date`, the DSR's predictions, staffing and the campaign's own result read it; a campaign is one label and one measurement (`campaign_effect`). Since the re-audit fix round (9/29/26): a holiday is keyed by its calendar identity (`holiday_key`, matched exactly — New Year's Day never reads New Year's Eve); a night that carried two things is `confounded`, a label is measured on its own nights where it has enough, and `effects_for_day` never multiplies two labels measured on the same nights (the larger applies, the other is `subsumed`); the median is age-weighted and a record whose last 3 nights moved says "was +25%, the last 3 nights +8%". A night carrying an if-necessary game with no result yet (`event_intel.store.unresolved_refs`) is left unmeasured, its rows removed, until a result or a cancellation is entered (event re-audit 10/1/26). Since event re-audit 2 (10/1/26): a label is matched to recorded nights by `label_counts` — every asked word in the row, and no season class word (preseason, playoff, special: `SEASON_CLASS_WORDS`) the asked label lacks — so a regular-season game's record never reads a preseason or playoff night, nor the reverse; a label summary leaves out confounded nights by `event_intel.store.clean_first`, the same rule as `effect_for`; and the boot's `rekey_record` reads only the rows recorded since its last pass (its `job_cursors` marker `event_memory_rekeyed_through`). The sales forecast also corrects itself from its own misses (`demand.forecast_calibration`), the prep list and Food Cost's stock-out and order projections read the measured effects of the order window and this restaurant's own week (`inventory.usage_profile`, `order_window_effects`), and the supplier order comes down for waste once. It also keeps the restaurant's public history: its own Google rating week by week, competitors arriving, leaving and moving — each stamped on the restaurant's own day (`record_own_rating` / `record_market_snapshot` default to its local now, never the server's UTC one).

**One reader per stored fact** (`canonical_facts`): final days only (a night the POS had not closed is provisional; a converted demo's seeded day is none), the one nightly net with its basis (the report's own net or a POS total built the same way — `dsr_net` — against any other POS's daily total, `pos_daily_total`; two figures are compared only on one basis), last year in the DSR's order (the report, the owner's imported workbook, the POS) naming its source, live reviews on the one time axis, reach that is reach alone, watched nights, published weeks, and the kind every stored figure is (measured, forecast, estimate, assumed, seeded). `tests/test_mem_m5_canonical_facts.py` holds every learner to it from the source.

**Who may teach** (`models.learning_eligible`): a demo, a test account, an internal account, an account an admin excluded (`learning_override`) and a converted demo's demo era (`learning_since`) teach no pooled learner. **Who learns for itself** (`models.learns_for_itself`, memory re-audit 9/29/26): every restaurant but a demo or an admin's exclusion — a test-named or internal account keeps its own ranking, voice, patterns, corrections, event memory and nightly pass (claims, trackers, thresholds, scorecard), and a paying test-named account raises an admin issue. An admin's view-as answer, save or approval is recorded with its authority and teaches nothing either.

**What outlives a prune.** `change_log` is the lasting, attributed history of settings, targets, prices, menu, roster and hours (who — owner, manager, admin, sync, seeded — and when), append-only and in no retention registry; `models.update_restaurant` writes it inside its own transaction, and the roster, menu, price, par and goal writers call `record`. It answers "which labor target applied last March" (`value_as_of`, read by Ask's `read_target_history`), gives the "changed since" caution its who (`rec_trust.owner_changes`) and gives outcomes their concurrent changes. `history_rollups` writes, before raw rows go, summaries kept forever with no guest text: `alert_monthly`, `engagement_monthly`, newsletter results stamped at 30 days, `review_monthly_stats` (in the review purge's own transaction), `inventory_weekly_summary` and `ingredient_cost_monthly`; a month is rolled only while its raw rows are all there. The prune runs each table's rollup first (`ops._RETENTION_ROLLUP`) and refuses a window set below its floor.

## Marketing tags and post attribution (`marketing_tags.py`, `marketing_signals.py`)

Every content-log row carries what the post was about — `menu_item_id`
(only a dish the restaurant has), `occasion` (game day, holiday, event,
offer, weather, weekend) and `post_kind` — inferred from the topic and
caption, correctable by the owner. Attribution reads a post's window
beyond total sales: the promoted dish's own units against the same
weekdays before (`menu_item_sales`), reviews in the next fortnight that
named it, the guest list's move, and engagement. The summary shows what did
not land next to what did, and medians by kind, occasion and dish. A
publish starts the month's observed sales tracker (`post_published`).

Bands (confidence audit 9/24/26): a post's lift is a move only past
t(90%, n−1) × the standard error of window-mean minus baseline-mean
(`noise_band_pct`, floor 5%, `false_alarm_rate` 0.10), and only with a
baseline spanning ≥2 different weeks; the promoted dish's unit lift carries
its own band and `item_verdict`; a group median carries `verdicts` and a
`verdict` that is "lifted" only when most of its posts lifted; a
period-on-period change % needs ≥3 posts in each period (`change_note`
says why otherwise).
